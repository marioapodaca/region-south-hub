SLF Standard 5: Family and Community Engagement

Standard 5 addresses how principals create and sustain meaningful partnerships with the families and communities that schools serve. Family and community engagement is not a communication function — it is a partnership function. The distinction matters: communication sends information outward, while partnership invites families into shared decision-making, shared learning, and shared ownership of student success.

This standard is organized into two components: 5A (Engages families and community members as partners in student success) and 5B (Creates or maintains school-home-community communication structures). Component 5A addresses the depth and authenticity of family partnership. Component 5B addresses the systems and structures that make communication equitable, consistent, and culturally responsive. Together they describe a school that families want to be part of — not one they are merely informed about.

Component 5A: Engages Families and Community Members as Partners in Student Success

This component addresses whether families are genuinely included as partners in their children's education — not just recipients of school communications. Authentic family partnership requires cultural responsiveness, accessible opportunities for participation, meaningful roles in school governance, and community connections that extend the school's reach beyond its walls. The key question throughout 5A is: do families have real power and voice in this school, or do they simply receive information about it?

5A.1
Promotes Family Involvement in Student Learning
Creates and maintains accessible, culturally responsive opportunities for families to be actively involved in supporting their children's learning — at school and at home — and ensures that most families participate in meaningful ways.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Creates and maintains accessible, culturally responsive opportunities for family involvement in student learning both at school and at home.
Communicates clearly to families how they can support their children's learning at home.
Most families participate in school activities and feel welcomed and respected.
At the Highly Effective level: Nearly all families participate in multiple meaningful ways; families report feeling genuine ownership over their children's education; family knowledge and expertise are actively incorporated into learning experiences.
No evidence for Promoting Family Involvement in Student Learning observed.
Equity lens: Effective family involvement in Region South schools must be culturally and linguistically responsive. Events held only in English, during work hours, or without childcare communicate that some families are more welcome than others. An Effective principal actively removes these barriers and tracks participation by demographic group to identify who is not yet engaged.
Examples of Evidence
Attendance data from family events disaggregated by subgroup (EL families, SWD families)
Parent/family workshops in languages spoken in the school community
Family engagement calendar showing varied times, formats, and topics
Take-home learning materials, literacy kits, or family math night resources
Parent surveys on welcoming climate, accessibility, and quality of engagement opportunities
Evidence of removing participation barriers: childcare, translation, flexible scheduling
Family leadership in classroom volunteering, tutoring, or mentoring programs
Ineffective
Creates few or no accessible opportunities for family involvement in student learning; does not communicate effectively to families how they can support their children at home; few families participate in school activities or feel welcomed.
Developing
Creates some opportunities for family involvement in student learning, but with limited cultural responsiveness or accessibility; some families participate in school activities; communication about home support is inconsistent.
Effective
Creates and maintains accessible, culturally responsive opportunities for family involvement in student learning at school and at home; communicates clearly to families how they can support their children's learning; most families participate in school activities and feel welcomed and respected.
Highly Effective
Systematically creates and sustains accessible, culturally responsive opportunities for family involvement; nearly all families participate in multiple meaningful ways and report feeling genuine ownership over their children's education; family knowledge and expertise are actively incorporated into school learning experiences.
5A.2
Provides Opportunities for Family and Community Education
Provides or facilitates access to educational opportunities for families and community members that address their needs and interests — including academic support, digital literacy, health and wellness, immigration resources, and post-secondary pathways.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Provides or facilitates access to educational opportunities for families and community members that address identified needs and interests.
Opportunities are responsive to families' linguistic, cultural, and economic contexts.
Most families who participate in educational programming report that it is relevant and useful.
At the Highly Effective level: Educational opportunities are co-designed with families based on assessed needs; nearly all families have access to and utilize educational programming; programming builds family capacity to advocate for and support their children.
No evidence for Providing Opportunities for Family and Community Education observed.
Examples of Evidence
ESL, digital literacy, FAFSA/CADAA workshops for families
Parent University or similar structured family learning program
Community partners providing health, legal, or social services on campus
Family needs assessment used to design programming
Attendance and satisfaction data from family education events
Post-secondary readiness workshops for families of secondary students (A-G, financial aid, college application support)
Immigration and know-your-rights resources provided in accessible formats
Ineffective
Does not provide or facilitate access to educational opportunities for families or community members; family learning needs are not assessed or addressed.
Developing
Provides limited or infrequent educational opportunities for families; opportunities may not be responsive to families' linguistic, cultural, or economic contexts; participation is low.
Effective
Provides or facilitates access to educational opportunities for families and community members that address identified needs and interests; opportunities are responsive to families' linguistic, cultural, and economic contexts; most families who participate report that programming is relevant and useful.
Highly Effective
Educational opportunities are co-designed with families based on systematically assessed needs; nearly all families have access to and utilize educational programming; programming builds family capacity to advocate for and support their children across all aspects of their schooling.
5A.3
Involves Families in School Governance and Decision-Making
Creates authentic governance structures that give families and community members genuine voice and decision-making power over matters that affect their children's education — including budget, curriculum, school safety, and improvement planning.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Creates or maintains governance structures that provide families and community members with genuine opportunities to participate in school decision-making.
Families are represented on school governance bodies (School Site Council, ELAC, DAC/DELAC) and their input meaningfully influences school decisions.
Governance participation reflects the demographic diversity of the school community.
At the Highly Effective level: Families and community members co-lead governance processes; family input is systematically integrated into all major school decisions; governance bodies reflect the full diversity of the school community and operate with genuine shared authority.
No evidence for Involving Families in School Governance and Decision-Making observed.
Compliance vs. partnership: School Site Council and ELAC meetings are required — but meeting the requirement does not reach Effective. An Effective principal ensures that family members on governance bodies have real information, real choices, and real influence. Meetings where the agenda is pre-determined and families rubber-stamp administrator decisions are Developing practice regardless of attendance.
Examples of Evidence
School Site Council (SSC) meeting minutes showing family members raising and influencing agenda items
ELAC minutes showing EL family input incorporated into program decisions
SPSA developed with authentic family participation, not just signatures
Demographic analysis of governance body membership vs. school population
Evidence that family-raised concerns resulted in documented school changes
Training or capacity-building provided to family governance members
Ineffective
Does not create or maintain governance structures that provide families with genuine decision-making opportunities; family input is absent or disregarded; governance bodies do not reflect the school community's demographics.
Developing
Maintains required governance structures but with limited authentic family decision-making power; family input is sought but inconsistently incorporated; governance participation may not reflect community demographics.
Effective
Creates or maintains governance structures that provide families and community members with genuine decision-making opportunities; families are represented on governance bodies and their input meaningfully influences school decisions; governance participation reflects the demographic diversity of the school community.
Highly Effective
Families and community members co-lead governance processes; family input is systematically integrated into all major school decisions; governance bodies reflect the full diversity of the school community and operate with genuine shared authority over matters that affect student learning and school culture.
5A.4
Establishes and Maintains Community Partnerships
Identifies, establishes, and maintains partnerships with community organizations, businesses, and agencies that extend learning opportunities, provide student and family supports, and strengthen the school's connection to the community it serves.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Identifies and establishes partnerships with community organizations, businesses, or agencies that extend learning opportunities or provide student and family supports.
Partnerships are sustained over time and have demonstrable impact on student learning or family well-being.
Community partners are integrated into the school's work — not peripheral or occasional.
At the Highly Effective level: Community partnerships are strategically selected and sustained based on school data and family needs; partners play meaningful roles in instruction, mentoring, career development, or family support; students and families can name and access community resources.
No evidence for Establishing and Maintaining Community Partnerships observed.
Examples of Evidence
Partnership agreements with community organizations, businesses, nonprofits, or agencies
Evidence of partners providing tutoring, mentoring, health services, or career exploration on campus
Participation data from community-partner-led programs
University, community college, or workforce partnerships tied to A-G completion or career pathways
Community partners presenting at family events or contributing to family education programming
Evidence of sustained, multi-year partnerships vs. one-time events
Ineffective
Does not identify or maintain partnerships with community organizations; community resources are not leveraged in support of student learning or family well-being.
Developing
Establishes some partnerships with community organizations, but with limited sustainability or impact; community partners play peripheral or one-time roles in school programming.
Effective
Identifies, establishes, and maintains partnerships with community organizations, businesses, and agencies that extend learning opportunities and provide student and family supports; partnerships are sustained over time and have demonstrable impact on student learning or family well-being; community partners are integrated into the school's work.
Highly Effective
Community partnerships are strategically selected and sustained based on school data and assessed family needs; partners play meaningful, ongoing roles in instruction, mentoring, career development, or family support; students and families can readily identify and access community resources through the school.

Component 5B: Creates or Maintains School-Home-Community Communication Structures

This component addresses the systems and structures that make communication with families and the broader community equitable, consistent, and two-way. Communication structures are not just what the school sends out — they are how the school listens, responds, and adapts based on what it hears. An Effective principal's communication systems are accessible in multiple languages, reach all families (not just the most engaged), and create genuine channels for family input.

5B.1
Uses Multiple Communication Strategies to Reach All Families
Implements a multi-channel, multilingual communication system that ensures all families — including those who are hardest to reach — receive timely, relevant information about their children's learning, school programs, and opportunities.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Implements a multi-channel communication system — including digital, print, and in-person — that reaches all families.
Communications are provided in the languages spoken in the school community.
Communication is timely, regular, and includes both positive news and challenges.
Families have accessible, responsive ways to reach the school and receive timely responses.
At the Highly Effective level: Communication is systematically monitored for reach and effectiveness; family feedback is used to improve communication systems; hard-to-reach families are proactively identified and contacted through additional outreach.
No evidence for Using Multiple Communication Strategies to Reach All Families observed.
Examples of Evidence
School website, ParentSquare, School Messenger, robo-calls — multi-channel system documented
Communications translated into languages of the school community
Outreach records for families with low engagement — home visits, community liaisons
Response rate data for parent surveys or school communications
Evidence of two-way communication — school responds to family inquiries within defined timeframes
Family communication log maintained by office staff
Communication audit showing which families are not receiving or responding to school messages
Ineffective
Does not implement a multi-channel communication system; communications are infrequent, limited in language access, or one-way; many families do not receive or cannot access school communications; family inquiries are not responded to in a timely manner.
Developing
Implements some multi-channel communication strategies, but with inconsistent language access or reach; some families are not receiving school communications; response to family inquiries is inconsistent.
Effective
Implements a multi-channel, multilingual communication system that reaches most families; communications are timely, regular, and include both positive news and challenges; families have accessible, responsive ways to reach the school and receive timely responses.
Highly Effective
Communication system is systematically monitored for reach and effectiveness; family feedback is used to continuously improve communication; hard-to-reach families are proactively identified and receive targeted outreach; nearly all families report feeling informed and able to access the school.
5B.2
Promotes Positive and Productive Relationships Between School and Community
Actively cultivates trust-based relationships between the school and the broader community — including local organizations, media, elected officials, and community leaders — that contribute to the school's reputation as an anchor institution and resource for the community it serves.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Actively cultivates trust-based relationships with the broader community beyond families of currently enrolled students.
The school is known and respected as a community asset — a place community members want to support and partner with.
Principal responds to community concerns proactively and transparently, maintaining the school's reputation and community trust.
At the Highly Effective level: The school is a recognized anchor institution in the community; principal leverages community relationships to expand resources and opportunities for students; community members actively advocate for the school.
No evidence for Promoting Positive and Productive Relationships Between School and Community observed.
Examples of Evidence
Principal participates in community events, neighborhood coalitions, or local government meetings
Media coverage of school highlighting community partnership and student success
Letters of support or partnership from community organizations
Community members and business leaders present at school events or serve as mentors
School featured positively in community communications or social media
Evidence of principal addressing community concerns directly (e.g., town halls, neighborhood meetings)
School open to community use after hours — library, gym, garden, meeting spaces
Ineffective
Does not actively cultivate relationships with the broader community; the school has a negative or unknown reputation in the community; community concerns are not addressed proactively or transparently.
Developing
Maintains some relationships with the broader community, but primarily reactively; the school has a neutral or inconsistent reputation; community concerns are addressed when they arise but without consistent proactive outreach.
Effective
Actively cultivates trust-based relationships with the broader community; the school is known and respected as a community asset; principal responds to community concerns proactively and transparently, maintaining the school's reputation and community trust.
Highly Effective
The school is a recognized anchor institution in the community; the principal strategically leverages community relationships to expand resources and opportunities for students; community members actively advocate for the school and contribute to its mission.
