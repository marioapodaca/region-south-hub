Standard 4: Additional Professional Responsibilities

Standard 4 addresses the responsibilities teachers carry beyond the classroom door — maintaining accurate records, communicating with families, and demonstrating professionalism. These responsibilities directly support student learning and build community trust. Like Standard 1, Standard 4 is assessed through professional conversations and artifacts rather than classroom observation. It encompasses three components: maintaining accurate records (4a), communicating with families (4b), and demonstrating professionalism (4c).

📝 Assessed through: Professional Conversations (PC) and Artifacts (A) — not Classroom Observation

Component 4a: Maintaining Accurate Records

Accurate records inform interactions with parents, students, and administrators. They inform practice and make teachers more responsive to individual student needs by tracking student growth over time. Instructional records include student assignments, skill lists, records of competencies, grades, and portfolios. Non-instructional records include attendance registers, field trip permission slips, picture money, supply orders, book orders, lunch records, and discipline referrals. Teachers should use available technology for record keeping. Efficiency of operation in record keeping is a key to success — well-designed and implemented systems require very little ongoing maintenance.

4a1
Tracks Progress Towards Identified Learning Outcomes
Teacher monitors how students are progressing toward the identified learning outcomes. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher has a well-organized system for maintaining information on student progress toward learning outcomes.
System allows for tracking student growth over time.
At the Highly Effective level: System allows for tracking individual student growth over time. Students contribute information and participate in maintaining records indicating their own progress.
No evidence for Tracking Progress Towards Identified Learning Outcomes observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Tracks Progress Towards Identified Learning Outcomes (4a1) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. This element connects directly to the data-driven instruction work central to Region South's instructional leadership — it asks whether teachers have a systematic, organized approach to monitoring student progress, not just whether they assign grades.
Ineffective
Teacher has no system for maintaining information on student progress in learning. Student progress cannot be tracked.
Developing
Teacher's system for maintaining information on student progress in learning is rudimentary and only partially effective. Student progress is inconsistently or randomly tracked.
Effective
Teacher system for maintaining information on student progress is well organized and tracks student progress towards learning outcomes. System allows for tracking student growth over time.
Highly Effective
Teacher system for maintaining information on student progress is well organized, efficient, and tracks student progress towards learning outcomes. System allows for tracking individual student growth over time. Students contribute information and participate in maintaining records indicating their own progress in learning.
4a2
Tracks Completion of Student Assignments in Support of Student Learning
Teacher keeps track of student assignments in support of student learning. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher's system for maintaining information on student completion of assignments is organized.
At the Highly Effective level: System is highly organized and efficient. Students participate in maintaining the records.
No evidence for Tracking Completion of Student Assignments observed.
Ineffective
Teacher has no system for maintaining information on student completion of assignments.
Developing
Teacher's system for maintaining information on student completion of assignments is rudimentary and only partially organized.
Effective
Teacher's system for maintaining information on student completion of assignments is organized.
Highly Effective
Teacher's system for maintaining information on student completion of assignments is highly organized and efficient. Students participate in maintaining the records.
4a3
Manages Non-Instructional Records
Teacher accurately manages records such as health records, book inventories, field trip permission slips, lunch counts, etc. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher maintains an organized system for managing information on non-instructional activities.
At the Highly Effective level: System is highly organized and students contribute to its management.
No evidence for Management of Non-Instructional Records observed.
Ineffective
Teacher has no system of maintaining records for non-instructional activities, resulting in errors and confusion.
Developing
Teacher's records for non-instructional activities are adequate but require frequent monitoring to avoid errors.
Effective
Teacher maintains an organized system for managing information on non-instructional activities.
Highly Effective
Teacher maintains a highly organized system for managing information on non-instructional activities, and students contribute to its management.
4a4
Submits Records on Time
Teacher submits records within the expected timeline. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher's submission of documents is always accurate, timely, and complete.
At the Highly Effective level: Submissions are always accurate, timely, complete, and provide contextual details when appropriate.
No evidence for Timely Submission of Records observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Submits Records on Time (4a4) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. Timely submission of required documents — grades, attendance, IEP-related records, and district-required reports — is a professional baseline that enables school and district operations to function and protects student rights.
Ineffective
Teacher's submission of documents is late, incomplete, or absent.
Developing
Teacher's submission of documents is usually on time and complete, but reminders may be necessary.
Effective
Teacher's submission of documents is always accurate, timely, and complete.
Highly Effective
Teacher's submission of documents is always accurate, timely, complete, and provides contextual details when appropriate.

Component 4b: Communicating with Families

Parents and guardians care deeply about the progress of their child and appreciate meaningful communication regarding their child's progress and achievement. Communication should include personal contact that will establish positive and ongoing two-way communications. Effective family communication goes beyond report cards and parent conferences — it reflects cultural sensitivity, responsiveness to family concerns, and proactive outreach about both challenges and opportunities.

4b1
Information About the Instructional Program
The teacher informs families about the instructional program in order to be clear about the learning expectations. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher provides required and additional information on a regular basis to families about the instructional program.
Teacher uses technology and other communication means to enhance parent-teacher communication.
At the Highly Effective level: Students participate in preparing materials for their families. Teacher regularly uses technology to enhance two-way parent-teacher communication.
No evidence for Communication of Information About the Instructional Program observed.
Ineffective
Teacher provides little or no information about the instructional program to families. Teacher does not utilize technology or any other communication means to enhance parent-teacher communication.
Developing
Teacher provides required information but offers little additional information about the instructional program to families. Teacher uses technology minimally and/or other communication means to enhance parent-teacher communication.
Effective
Teacher provides required and additional information on a regular basis to families about the instructional program. Teacher uses technology and other communication means to enhance parent-teacher communication.
Highly Effective
Teacher provides required and ongoing additional information to families about the instructional program. Students participate in preparing materials for their families. Teacher regularly uses technology and other communication means to enhance two-way parent-teacher communication.
4b2
Information About Individual Students
The teacher is able to respond to families about the progress of their own child(ren). Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher communicates with families about students' progress on a regular basis, beyond report cards and parent conferences.
Communication demonstrates sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences.
Teacher is available as needed to respond to family concerns and communicates available resources or interventions.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher is proactive in communicating with families regarding resources, interventions, and enrichment opportunities. Responses to family concerns are handled expeditiously.
No evidence for Communication of Information About Individual Students observed.
Ineffective
Teacher provides minimal information to families about individual students, or the communication is inappropriate to the cultures of the families. Responses to family concerns are not handled with professional and cultural sensitivity.
Developing
Teacher adheres to the school's required procedures for communicating with families. Responses to family concerns are minimal or may reflect occasional insensitivity to cultural norms or language differences.
Effective
Teacher communicates with families about students' progress on a regular basis, beyond report cards and parent conferences, demonstrating sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences. Teacher is available as needed to respond to family concerns. Teacher communicates available resources or interventions.
Highly Effective
Teacher frequently communicates with families about students' progress, beyond report cards and parent conferences, demonstrating sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences. Responses to family concerns are handled expeditiously. Teacher is proactive in communicating with families regarding resources, interventions, and enrichment opportunities.
4b3
Engagement of Families in the Instructional Program
The teacher communicates with families to create a partnership around student learning. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher engages families in the instructional program in multiple ways including the use of technology and/or assignments that invite family input.
Teacher demonstrates sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher frequently engages families in the instructional program and encourages families to contribute ideas that enhance family participation in support of student learning.
No evidence for Engagement of Families in the Instructional Program observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Engagement of Families in the Instructional Program (4b3) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. Note: For EDST, 4b3 may also be collected as evidence for Contributions to School Community (CSC). The key distinction at Effective vs. Highly Effective is whether the teacher invites family input that shapes the program — not just communicates information to families.
Ineffective
Teacher makes no attempt to engage families in the instructional program or teacher's attempts are insensitive to cultural norms and language differences.
Developing
Teacher makes limited attempts to engage families in the instructional program. Teacher's attempts inconsistently demonstrate sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences.
Effective
Teacher engages families in the instructional program in multiple ways including the use of technology and/or assignments that invite family input. Teacher demonstrates sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences.
Highly Effective
Teacher frequently engages families in the instructional program in multiple ways including the use of technology and/or assignments that invite family input. Teacher encourages families to contribute ideas that enhance family participation in support of student learning. Teacher demonstrates sensitivity to cultural norms and language differences.

Component 4c: Demonstrating Professionalism

Teaching professionals display the highest standards of integrity and ethical conduct; they are intellectually honest and conduct themselves in ways consistent with a comprehensive moral code. Educators recognize that the purpose of schools is to educate students and embrace a responsibility to ensure that every student will learn. Teachers are keenly alert to and advocate for the needs of their students. Educators demonstrate a commitment to professional standards, problem solving, and decision-making. Professional educators comply with school, district, state, and federal regulations and procedures.

4c1
Ethical Conduct and Compliance with School, District, State, and Federal Regulations
The teacher exhibits integrity and ethical conduct in all interactions with the school and community and complies with all rules and regulations of the profession. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher displays high standards of honesty, integrity, discretion, and confidentiality in interactions with colleagues, students, parents, the school community, and the public.
Teacher supports and fully complies with school and district regulations.
Teacher models professional demeanor.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher takes a leadership role in establishing and articulating school and district regulations to others.
No evidence for Ethical Conduct and Regulatory Compliance observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Ethical Conduct and Compliance (4c1) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. This is one of the few TLF elements where Ineffective practice may warrant immediate administrative action rather than a coaching response. An Effective teacher does not merely comply with regulations — they model professional demeanor and discretion in all community interactions.
Ineffective
Teacher displays dishonesty in interactions with colleagues, students, parents, the school community, and the public, or teacher does not comply with school and district regulations.
Developing
Teacher is honest in interactions with colleagues, students, parents, the school community, and the public. Teacher partially complies with school and district regulations or is inconsistent in modeling a professional demeanor.
Effective
Teacher displays high standards of honesty, integrity, discretion, and confidentiality in interactions with colleagues, students, parents, the school community, and the public. Teacher supports and fully complies with school and district regulations and models professional demeanor.
Highly Effective
Teacher displays high standards of honesty, integrity, discretion, and confidentiality, and takes a leadership role with colleagues, students, parents, the school community, and the public. Teacher supports and fully complies with school and district regulations, models professional demeanor, and takes a leadership role in establishing and articulating such regulations.
4c2
Advocacy for Students
The teacher is aware of students' needs and advocates for all students, particularly those who may be underserved. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher is aware of student needs and actively works to ensure that all students receive an opportunity to succeed.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher is highly proactive in challenging negative attitudes or practices and seeks out resources for students who are traditionally underserved.
No evidence for Advocacy for Students observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Advocacy for Students (4c2) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. This element is at the heart of Region South's equity work. An Effective teacher actively works to ensure opportunity — not just access. A Highly Effective teacher proactively challenges systemic practices that disadvantage students and seeks out resources for those historically underserved.
Ineffective
Teacher is not alert to student needs and contributes to school practices that result in some students being ill-served by the school.
Developing
Teacher is partially aware of student needs and attempts to address practices that result in some students being ill-served by the school.
Effective
Teacher is aware of student needs and actively works to ensure that all students receive an opportunity to succeed.
Highly Effective
Teacher is aware of student needs and is highly proactive in challenging negative attitudes or practices to ensure that all students, particularly those traditionally underserved, are honored in the school, seeking out resources as needed.
4c3
Decision-Making
The teacher is comfortable making informed decisions related to the wellbeing of students and student learning. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher maintains an open mind and collaborates in team or departmental decision-making.
Teacher's decisions are based on thorough, genuinely professional considerations.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher takes a leadership role in team or departmental decision-making and helps ensure that such decisions are based on the highest professional standards.
No evidence for Professional Decision-Making observed.
Ineffective
Teacher makes decisions and recommendations based on self-serving interests.
Developing
Teacher's decisions and recommendations are based on limited, though genuinely professional, considerations.
Effective
Teacher maintains an open mind and collaborates in team or departmental decision-making. Teacher's decisions are based on thorough, genuinely professional considerations.
Highly Effective
Teacher takes a leadership role in team or departmental decision-making and helps ensure that such decisions are based on the highest professional standards.
