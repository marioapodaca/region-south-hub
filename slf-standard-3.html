SLF Standard 3: Instruction

School leaders demonstrate instructional leadership by supporting teachers in improving their instruction, developing curriculum, building their capacity to analyze data, and taking on leadership responsibilities. Standard 3 is the SLF standard most directly connected to instructional supervision and principal evaluation. When region leadership walks classrooms alongside the principal, they are simultaneously observing teacher instruction through the TLF lens and observing the principal's instructional leadership through the SLF Standard 3 lens. Component 3C — Assesses the Performance of All Staff — contains the two unified focus elements that apply to every principal's observation of practice rating.

Observation of Practice Rating — Unified Focus Elements

The SLF requires that principal evaluations include 3 unified focus elements plus up to 2 cooperatively-established elements. Two of those 3 unified focus elements come from Standard 3, Component 3C. These are the elements region leadership is most directly assessing during school site visits — during classroom walkthroughs and in the post-visit debrief.

Component 3A: Provides Support for Teachers to Improve Instruction

The principal uses objective evidence from observations of teacher practice and student data to identify teacher strengths and needs, and ensures that differentiated support and coaching are provided based on individual and group needs and strengths.

3A.1
Implements Coaching and Support
The principal uses objective evidence from observations of teacher practice and student data to identify teacher strengths and needs, and ensures that differentiated support and coaching are provided.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Uses objective evidence from observations of teacher practice and student data to identify teacher strengths and needs within and across grade levels and subject areas.
Assists teachers and teams in identifying their own needs and strengths.
Ensures that differentiated support and coaching are provided based on individual and group needs and strengths.
Supports teachers in determining next steps that result in improved practice and progress toward goal attainment.
No evidence for Implementation of Coaching and Support observed.
Examples of Evidence
Pre- or post-conference notes in MyPGS MyTeam report generated re: overall evaluation results for an EDST Cycle Analysis of MyTeam report or action plan for addressing individual/group weaknesses Supervisor's observation of Growth Planning or other coaching conferences Individual coaching log/tracker by teacher Notes from group coaching sessions with school leader or instructional coach
Ineffective
Does not have a system in place to assess teacher strengths and needs in the absence of objective evidence from classroom observations or student data; does not provide individual or group coaching or support for struggling teachers to improve practice.
Developing
Uses limited objective evidence from observations of teacher practice and student data, or anecdotal evidence, to identify teacher strengths and needs; may subjectively identify low-performing teachers and provide targeted support and coaching for struggling teachers or teams to improve practice.
Effective
Uses objective evidence from observations of teacher practice and student data to identify teacher strengths and needs within and across grade levels and subject areas, and assists teachers and teams in identifying their own needs and strengths. Ensures that differentiated support and coaching are provided based on individual and group needs; supports teachers in determining next steps that result in improved practice and progress toward goal attainment.
Highly Effective
Systematically assesses each teacher's strengths and needs based on multiple forms of objective evidence collected from observations and student data. Ensures that differentiated support and ongoing coaching within and across grade levels and subject areas are provided based on individual and group needs, student learning needs, and school-wide goals; supports all instructional staff in determining next steps that result in improved practice and movement toward goal attainment; acknowledges and celebrates progress made in improved practice.
3A.2
Ensures High-Quality Professional Development That Results in Implementation of Effective Pedagogy
The principal provides professional development and resources to support teachers in modifying their pedagogy and implementing research-based, culturally and linguistically responsive pedagogical (CLRP) practices.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Provides professional development and resources to support teachers in modifying their pedagogy and implementing research-based, culturally and linguistically responsive pedagogical (CLRP) practices.
Professional development is focused, organized, well-presented, appropriately sequenced, differentiated, and designed and revised based on staff input.
Most teachers modify their instruction in ways consistent with the TLF.
No evidence for High-Quality Professional Development Leading to Effective Pedagogy observed.
Examples of Evidence
Notes/photos from PD, PLCs, team meetings Lesson study and data chat protocols PD Calendar and Revised PD Calendar Classroom student data (e.g., subgroups) demonstrating PD implementation School leader provides feedback to teachers re: instructional strategies seen in classrooms Evidence of sustained change over time in teachers' practice
Ineffective
Provides professional development that does not support teachers in modifying their pedagogy to meet the needs of students. Professional development is episodic, irregular, irrelevant, or poorly presented, and is not based on research-based practices or input from staff. Few teachers modify their instruction following professional development.
Developing
Provides professional development and resources to support teachers in modifying their pedagogy and implementing a limited number of research-based practices. Professional development is organized and well-presented, but with minimal differentiation or input from staff. Some teachers modify their instruction in ways consistent with the TLF.
Effective
Provides professional development and resources to support teachers in modifying their pedagogy and implementing research-based, culturally and linguistically responsive pedagogical (CLRP) practices, and best practices to enhance SEL. Professional development is focused, organized, well-presented, appropriately sequenced, differentiated, and designed and revised based on staff input. Most teachers modify their instruction in ways consistent with the TLF.
Highly Effective
Provides comprehensive professional development and resources to support teachers in consistently implementing research-based, CLRP practices and curricular content, and best practices to enhance SEL. Professional development is differentiated and includes modeling and facilitation of strategies consistent with adult learning theory; it is designed, led, and revised by site-based and other educators. Nearly all teachers modify their instruction in ways consistent with the TLF.
3A.3
Creates Collaboration Structures That Support Ongoing Development
The principal provides regular opportunities for teams of teachers to collaborate for their own professional growth and in support of student learning.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Provides regular opportunities for teams of teachers to collaborate for their own professional growth and in support of student learning.
Team structures are flexible; most teams focus on student learning and effectively use protocols, peer facilitation, and strategic data analysis.
Team structure and content is designed and implemented with the assistance of instructional staff.
No evidence for Collaboration Structures That Support Ongoing Development observed.
Examples of Evidence
Minutes, photos, or agendas from PLCs, grade-level-team meetings, department meetings Evidence of facilitated team meetings Evidence of training and support for facilitators PD Calendar Lists of teams structured around student needs ILT agendas, notes, plans
Ineffective
Rarely provides opportunities for teams of teachers to collaborate for their own professional growth or in support of student learning. Teams are formed around grade levels or subject areas and rarely focus on student learning. There is little evidence of protocol use, peer facilitation, or strategic data analysis.
Developing
Provides occasional opportunities for teams of teachers to collaborate for their own professional growth or in support of student learning. Team structures may be flexible; some teams focus on student learning and effectively use protocols, peer facilitation, or strategic data analysis.
Effective
Provides regular opportunities for teams of teachers to collaborate for their own professional growth and in support of student learning. Team structures are flexible; most teams focus on student learning and effectively use protocols, peer facilitation, and strategic data analysis. Team structure and content is designed and implemented with the assistance of instructional staff.
Highly Effective
Provides systematic opportunities for teams of teachers to collaborate for their own professional growth and in support of student learning. Teams are structured around student needs, and all teams focus on student learning, making effective use of protocols, peer facilitation, and strategic data analysis; teacher leaders receive training and support in peer facilitation. Teachers take responsibility for designing the structure and content of their team meetings.
3A.4
Builds Capacity of Staff to Analyze and Utilize Student Data
The principal provides teachers with access to student learning and demographic data, along with training and support in collecting and analyzing multiple forms of data, including student work.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Provides teachers with access to student learning and demographic data, along with training and support in collecting and analyzing multiple forms of data, including student work.
Most instructional staff obtain and analyze student data for their classes, identifying learning progress and gaps, and determining grouping, enrichment, and intervention opportunities.
No evidence for Building Capacity of Staff to Analyze and Utilize Student Data observed.
Examples of Evidence
Print-outs or screen-shots of student data Photos — teacher teams analyzing student data Intervention and enrichment plans Universal Access Plan Photo of Data Wall from meeting room Protocol to analyze student data at a PD Scheduled data chats
Ineffective
Provides teachers with little or no access to student data, or does not provide training and support in analyzing student data. Few instructional staff are able to obtain student data for their classes, or to use data to determine student learning progress, grouping, enrichment, or intervention for groups of students.
Developing
Provides teachers with access to limited student data, and minimal training and support in analyzing student data. Some instructional staff obtain student data for their classes, analyzing the data to identify learning progress or gaps, or determine grouping, enrichment, or intervention opportunities for some subgroups or content areas.
Effective
Provides teachers with access to student learning and demographic data, along with training and support in collecting and analyzing multiple forms of data, including student work. Most instructional staff obtain and analyze student data for their classes, identifying learning progress and gaps, and determining grouping, enrichment, and intervention opportunities within grade levels or content areas.
Highly Effective
Provides teachers with electronic access to student learning and demographic data, along with systematic training and support in collecting and analyzing multiple forms of data, including student work. Most instructional staff regularly access and analyze student learning data to identify learning progress and gaps and determine differentiation, grouping, enrichment, and intervention opportunities within and across grade levels and content areas.

Component 3B: Promotes Quality Teaching, Learning, and Leadership

The principal monitors the school's curricular program, creates and maintains distributed leadership opportunities, and identifies and develops teachers as leaders. This component describes the systemic conditions the principal creates to sustain instructional quality across the school.

3B.1
Monitors School-Wide Curriculum in Relation to Content Standards and Opportunity to Learn
The principal ensures that the school's curricular program is rigorous and linked to content standards, and that each student has an adequate opportunity to learn rigorous content in all academic subjects.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Ensures that the school's curricular program is rigorous and linked to content standards.
Each student has an adequate opportunity to learn rigorous content in all academic subjects.
Most teachers engage in opportunities to evaluate and revise standards-aligned lessons and instruction.
No evidence for Monitoring School-Wide Curriculum in Relation to Content Standards observed.
Examples of Evidence
Pre-observation notes/lesson plans showing alignment to standards Scope/Sequence and unit plans connected to content standards Observations of classrooms showing horizontal alignment with unit plans Observations of grade-level or department meetings showing teachers creating/revising lessons Evidence of CLRP and curriculum related to SEL
Ineffective
Rarely monitors the school's curricular program to ensure links to content standards, quality, or opportunities for students to learn rigorous content; teachers rarely participate in evaluation or revision of standards-aligned lessons and instruction.
Developing
Monitors the school's curricular program in some subjects or grade levels to ensure links to content standards, quality, and opportunities for most students to learn rigorous content; some teachers engage in opportunities to evaluate or revise standards-aligned lessons and instruction.
Effective
Ensures that the school's curricular program is rigorous and linked to content standards, and that each student has an adequate opportunity to learn rigorous content in all academic subjects; most teachers engage in opportunities to evaluate and revise standards-aligned lessons and instruction.
Highly Effective
Builds capacity of teachers to collectively ensure that the school's curricular program is rigorous and aligned to content standards, and that each student has ongoing opportunities to learn rigorous content in all academic subjects; nearly all teachers regularly engage in opportunities to evaluate and revise standards-aligned lessons and instruction.
3B.2
Creates and Maintains Distributed Leadership Opportunities
The principal has structures in place to create leadership opportunities for most instructional staff, recruits and places staff in leadership positions based on skill and interests, and provides direction, training, and support.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Has structures in place to create leadership opportunities for most instructional staff.
Recruits and places instructional staff in leadership positions based on skill and interests.
Provides direction, training, and support for staff in leadership positions.
No evidence for Creating and Maintaining Distributed Leadership Opportunities observed.
Examples of Evidence
Observation of staff participating in leadership opportunities Shared facilitation in multiple settings Leadership grid: which staff lead other staff, student, or parent groups, or serve in governance Discussion notes re: match between staff skill and PD topics Content/expert teams
Ineffective
Does not provide opportunities for, or cultivate, shared leadership; does not provide direction, training, or support for staff in leadership positions.
Developing
Occasionally creates leadership opportunities for instructional staff; may fill leadership positions based on interest rather than skill; provides limited direction, training, or support for instructional staff in leadership positions.
Effective
Has structures in place to create leadership opportunities for most instructional staff; recruits and places instructional staff in leadership positions based on skill and interests; provides direction, training, and support.
Highly Effective
Has structures in place to build leadership skills and create leadership opportunities for all instructional staff; strategically recruits and places instructional staff in leadership positions based on skill, interests, and collegial relationships.
3B.3
Identifies and Develops Teachers as Leaders
The principal has processes in place to assess teachers' leadership potential and build teacher leadership capacity, involves teachers as leaders, and works with teacher leaders to identify leadership opportunities.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Has processes in place to assess teachers' leadership potential and build teacher leadership capacity.
Involves teachers as leaders, and works with teacher leaders to identify leadership opportunities for them in the school and/or district.
No evidence for Identifying and Developing Teachers as Leaders observed.
Examples of Evidence
Adjunct duties or committees Teachers as instructional leadership team members Teachers facilitate or lead professional development Stated/published roles and responsibilities for teacher leaders Meeting notes/agendas in which teacher leaders participate
Ineffective
Does not assess teachers' leadership potential, and does not build their leadership capacity. Rarely, if ever, acknowledges or accepts teachers as leaders.
Developing
Minimally assesses teachers' leadership potential, and infrequently offers opportunities to build teacher leadership capacity. Accepts or involves teachers as leaders when opportunities are presented.
Effective
Has processes in place to assess teachers' leadership potential and build teacher leadership capacity. Involves teachers as leaders, and works with teacher leaders to identify leadership opportunities for them in the school and/or district.
Highly Effective
Has formal systems and programs in place to assess teachers' leadership potential and build teacher leadership capacity. Proactively seeks out and involves teacher leaders, engages and mentors them as leaders to build school-wide and/or district-wide teacher leadership opportunities.

Component 3C: Assesses the Performance of All Staff

The principal conducts formal and informal observations, provides actionable feedback to staff, and manages the evaluation process. This component contains the two unified focus elements for principal observation of practice ratings — the elements most directly assessed during school site visits and principal evaluation cycles.

3C.1
Conducts Formal and Informal Observations
The principal regularly conducts formal and informal observations, consistently collecting and sorting objective evidence aligned to teachers' goals and focus elements.
⭐ Unified Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Regularly conducts formal and informal observations, consistently collecting and sorting objective evidence aligned to teachers' goals and focus elements.
Has systems in place so that peers or leadership team members also conduct regular observations.
Differentiates focus and number of observations based on staff need.
No evidence for Conducting Formal and Informal Observations observed.
Unified Focus Element — 3C.1 is one of the three unified focus elements that appear on every principal's Observation of Practice Rating. During school site visits, supervisors have direct real-time evidence of 3C.1: how the principal conducts walkthroughs, what notes they take, whether their evidence is objective and aligned to TLF elements, and whether they differentiate their observation focus based on teacher need.
Key observable differences by performance level during Monday walkthroughs: Ineffective — principal does not systematically collect evidence, or evidence is subjective. Developing — principal collects some evidence but it is not consistently TLF-aligned. Effective — principal collects objective, TLF-aligned evidence and differentiates focus by teacher. Highly Effective — principal and peers systematically collect specific, comprehensive evidence aligned to teacher goals and school priorities.
Examples of Evidence
Scripted observation notes in MyPGS Notes from paired observations between supervisor and school leader Observation log or tracker Notes from leadership or site-based teams participating in instructional rounds Photos from classroom observations District Informal Observation Tool Reports
Ineffective
Infrequently conducts formal or informal observations for those staff in an evaluation year; observation evidence is subjective, inaccurately aligned, or not representative of teachers' classroom practices; observations are not differentiated based on staff need.
Developing
Conducts formal and informal observations for those staff in an evaluation year (or has systems in place to ensure observations occur) with some collection and sorting of objective evidence; observations are occasionally differentiated based on staff need.
Effective
Regularly conducts formal and informal observations, consistently collecting and sorting objective evidence aligned to teachers' goals and focus elements. Has systems in place so that peers or leadership team members also conduct regular observations. Differentiates focus and number of observations based on staff need.
Highly Effective
Regularly and systematically conducts formal and informal observations, collecting and sorting specific, comprehensive, objective evidence aligned to teachers' goals or school priorities. Has systems in place so that peers and leadership team members also conduct regular observations to improve practice. Differentiates focus, number, and timing of observations based on staff need.
3C.2
Provides Actionable Feedback to Staff
The principal consistently provides actionable feedback to most staff that is focused on student learning and professional growth, and that is evidence-based and promotes teacher reflection.
⭐ Unified Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Consistently provides actionable feedback to most staff focused on student learning and professional growth.
Has systems in place so that staff receive feedback from an effective peer or leadership team member.
Questions and feedback are evidence-based and promote teacher reflection.
Feedback may be at the individual or group level.
Most teachers demonstrate changes in instructional practice following feedback.
No evidence for Providing Actionable Feedback to Staff observed.
Unified Focus Element — 3C.2 is one of the three unified focus elements that appear on every principal's Observation of Practice Rating. The post-visit debrief is a direct evidence-generating event for 3C.2 — region leadership watches the principal provide feedback to teachers and can assess in real time whether that feedback is actionable, evidence-based, and promotes reflection versus praise/correction only.
What distinguishes performance levels in the debrief: Ineffective — feedback is non-existent, negative, or mandate-based without evidence. Developing — feedback is inconsistent, uses limited evidence, or is framed as advice to high-need staff only. Effective — feedback is actionable, evidence-based, promotes teacher reflection, and most teachers demonstrate changed practice. Highly Effective — feedback is systematic, specific to individual needs, linked to goals, and provided to both individuals and groups; most teachers show consistent, ongoing changes.
Examples of Evidence
School leader's pre- or post-conference notes from EDST Supervisor's observation of a pre- or post-conference between school leader and teacher Emails or informal notes to teachers following informal visits Memos to teams or staff following informal observations or instructional rounds Photos documenting changes in classrooms after feedback Screen shots from the District Informal Observation Tool
Ineffective
Rarely provides actionable or evidence-based feedback; feedback may be negative, conciliatory, or in the form of mandates. Feedback does not focus on professional growth to improve practice. Teachers do not demonstrate or articulate changes made in instructional practices.
Developing
Inconsistently provides actionable feedback to high-need staff, or to those staff in an evaluation year, that focuses on student learning or professional growth. Questions and feedback use limited evidence from observations or student learning data, or are framed as advice. Some teachers demonstrate changes in instructional practice following feedback.
Effective
Consistently provides actionable feedback to most staff that is focused on student learning and professional growth (and/or has systems in place so that staff receive feedback from an effective peer or leadership team member). Questions and feedback are evidence-based and promote teacher reflection. Feedback may be at the individual or group level. Most teachers demonstrate changes in instructional practice following feedback.
Highly Effective
Staff receive systematic actionable feedback from an effective peer or member of the leadership team that is specific to the needs of the individual and builds on observation evidence, student learning data, and self-assessment. Questions and feedback are evidence-based, are linked to staff members' goals or school priorities, and focus on student learning and professional growth. Feedback is provided to both individuals and groups. Most teachers demonstrate consistent and ongoing changes in instructional practice following feedback.
3C.3
Manages the Evaluation Process
The principal consistently utilizes established protocols for supervising most staff that are clear, transparent, thorough, consistent, and followed with fidelity. Completes required evaluations in a timely manner.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Consistently utilizes established protocols for supervising most staff that are clear, transparent, thorough, consistent, and followed with fidelity.
Completes required evaluations in a timely manner.
At the Highly Effective level: Protocols are collaboratively designed; all staff evaluations are completed in a timely manner.
No evidence for Managing the Evaluation Process observed.
Examples of Evidence
EDST MyPGS Reports demonstrating completion of EDST Cycles Notes from pre- or post-conferences Group-created or adapted protocols or resources around evaluation processes Supervision protocols and notes for classified staff
Ineffective
Established protocols for supervising teaching and learning are not followed with fidelity. Evaluations may be incomplete, even for teachers or classified staff in an evaluation year.
Developing
Utilizes established protocols for supervising teaching and learning and completes required evaluations with fidelity. Provides minimal supervision for teachers who are not in an evaluation year.
Effective
Consistently utilizes established protocols for supervising most staff that are clear, transparent, thorough, consistent, and followed with fidelity. Completes required evaluations in a timely manner.
Highly Effective
Systematically utilizes established protocols for supervising all staff that are clear, transparent, comprehensive, consistent, and collaboratively designed. Ensures that required evaluations for all staff are completed in a timely manner.
