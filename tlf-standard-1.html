Standard 1: Planning and Preparation

Standard 1 addresses what teachers know and how they plan before instruction begins. Unlike Standards 2 and 3, which are primarily observed in the classroom, Standard 1 is assessed through professional conversations, lesson and unit plans, assessments, and other artifacts. It encompasses five components: knowledge of content and pedagogy, knowledge of students, instructional outcomes, coherent instruction design, and student assessment design.

📝 Assessed through: Professional Conversations (PC) and Artifacts (A) — not Classroom Observation

Component 1a: Demonstrating Knowledge of Content and Pedagogy

Teachers must have sufficient command of the subject to guide student learning and they must also know how their content fits into a larger context. Since every discipline has its own approach to instruction, teachers need to tailor their pedagogy to their content. Knowledge of content and pedagogy is not stagnant, but evolves over time and requires on-going, collaborative learning to support 21st Century Skills and learners.

1a1
Knowledge of Content and the Structure of the Discipline
Knows the discipline and how the subjects within the discipline relate and integrate with one another. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher articulates knowledge of concepts in the discipline through the development of essential understandings and big ideas aligned to the standards.
Teacher demonstrates knowledge of the progression of content standards within and across adjacent grade levels.
Teacher makes connections across grade levels and disciplines.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher articulates advanced knowledge across multiple grade levels and disciplines, connecting essential understandings to standards across content areas.
No evidence for Knowledge of Content and the Structure of the Discipline observed.
Evidence sources: Lesson plans, unit plans, professional conversations about content — not classroom observation. Evidence of how the teacher understands and communicates the structure of the discipline.
Ineffective
Teacher makes content errors or the teacher's plan does not articulate sufficient knowledge of the content standards.
Developing
Teacher articulates a basic knowledge of the grade-level concepts in the discipline. Teacher demonstrates limited connections across grade levels.
Effective
Teacher articulates knowledge of the concepts in the discipline through the development of essential understandings and big ideas that are aligned to the standards. Teacher demonstrates knowledge of the progression of the content standards within and across adjacent grade levels.
Highly Effective
Teacher articulates advanced knowledge of the concepts in the discipline through the development of essential understandings and big ideas that are aligned to standards across disciplines. Teacher demonstrates knowledge of the progression of the content standards within and across multiple grade levels and disciplines.
1a2
Knowledge of Content-Related Pedagogy
Uses pedagogical content knowledge to plan instruction in the particular subject area. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDST Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher's plan reflects intentional selection of effective research-based pedagogical approaches appropriate to the discipline.
Plan is appropriate for the essential understandings and big ideas addressed in the lesson.
Teacher plans appropriate use of technology and 21st Century Skills.
Teacher anticipates students' misunderstandings and plans how to address them.
No evidence for Knowledge of Content-Related Pedagogy observed.
EDST Focus Element — Knowledge of Content-Related Pedagogy (1a2) is a focus element for the EDST evaluation cycle. The key distinction is intentionality: an Effective teacher deliberately selects pedagogical approaches grounded in research on how this specific content is learned, anticipates misconceptions, and plans to address them.
Ineffective
Teacher's plan displays little or no understanding of appropriate pedagogical approaches suitable to the essential understandings and big ideas that should be addressed in the lesson.
Developing
Teacher's plan reflects limited pedagogical approaches or some approaches that are not suitable to the essential understandings and big ideas addressed in the lesson. Teacher's plan incorporates limited connections to 21st Century Skills.
Effective
Teacher's plan reflects intentional selection of effective research-based pedagogical approaches in the discipline and is appropriate for the essential understandings and big ideas addressed in the lesson. Teacher plans appropriate use of technology and 21st Century Skills. Teacher anticipates students' misunderstandings.
Highly Effective
Teacher's plan reflects intentional selection of effective research-based pedagogical approaches, appropriate for the essential understanding and big ideas addressed in the lesson. Teacher plans appropriate use of technology and 21st Century Skills. Teacher anticipates students' misunderstandings and plans how to address them.

Component 1b: Demonstrating Knowledge of Students

It is not enough for teachers to know and understand childhood or adolescent developmental norms; teachers must also know their students: their strengths and weaknesses, their interests, their readiness levels and skill sets, their language proficiency, and the outside influences — including exposure to trauma — that affect their social emotional learning, family dynamics, cultural customs, and socio-economics.

1b1
Awareness of Students' Skills, Knowledge, and Language Proficiency
Uses information about students' academic strengths and needs in planning. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDST + EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher's plan tracks learners' skills, knowledge, and language proficiency.
Teacher articulates knowledge of the class as a whole and disaggregates data for subgroups of learners or individuals.
Data disaggregation is used to determine progress and to plan instruction and intervention.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher tracks individuals' skills, knowledge, and language proficiency and disaggregates data for subgroups and each individual learner to plan instruction.
No evidence for Awareness of Students' Skills, Knowledge, and Language Proficiency observed.
EDST Focus Element and EDSNCT Focus Element — This is a unified focus element for both evaluation cycles. The critical distinction between Effective and Highly Effective is granularity: Effective teachers disaggregate for subgroups; Highly Effective teachers track individual student progress and plan accordingly.
Ineffective
Teacher's plan articulates little or no awareness of learners' skills, knowledge, and language proficiency, and/or does not indicate that such knowledge is valuable.
Developing
Teacher's plan articulates the value of tracking learners' skills, knowledge, and language proficiency, but applies this knowledge in planning only for the class/group as a whole.
Effective
Teacher's plan tracks learners' skills, knowledge, and language proficiency. Teacher articulates knowledge of the class/group as a whole and disaggregates data for subgroups of learners or individuals in order to determine progress and to plan instruction and intervention.
Highly Effective
Teacher's plan tracks individuals' skills, knowledge, and language proficiency. Teacher articulates knowledge of the class/group as a whole and disaggregates data for learner subgroups and individuals in order to determine progress for each learner and to plan instruction and intervention.
1b2
Knowledge of How Children, Adolescents, or Adults Learn
Plans lessons based on current, proven research regarding how students learn. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher's plans articulate current and accurate knowledge of how students learn, reflecting an understanding of the typical developmental characteristics of the age group.
Plans reflect understanding of students' social emotional needs and how 21st Century Skills apply.
Teacher applies developmental knowledge in planning for the class as a whole and for subgroups of learners.
No evidence for Knowledge of How Children, Adolescents, or Adults Learn observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Knowledge of How Children, Adolescents, or Adults Learn (1b2) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. Effective non-classroom teachers articulate both what they know about how their particular learners develop and how they use that knowledge in planning for subgroups.
Ineffective
Teacher's plans articulate little or no knowledge of the developmental characteristics of the age group, the social emotional needs, or of how these students learn.
Developing
Teacher's plans articulate some knowledge of the developmental characteristics of the age group and their social emotional needs. Teacher applies this knowledge in planning for the class/group as a whole.
Effective
Teacher's plans articulate current and accurate knowledge of how students learn, reflecting an understanding of the typical developmental characteristics of the age group, including their social emotional needs, and how 21st Century Skills apply. Teacher applies this knowledge in planning for the class/group as a whole and for subgroups of learners.
Highly Effective
Teacher's plans articulate current and accurate knowledge of how students learn, reflecting an understanding of the typical developmental characteristics of the age group, including their social emotional needs, and how 21st Century Skills apply. Teacher applies this knowledge in planning for the class/group as a whole, for subgroups of learners, and for individuals.
1b3
Knowledge of Students' Special Needs
Knows which students have special needs and plans instruction to accommodate for those needs. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher possesses information about students' learning and medical needs.
Teacher applies knowledge of special needs when planning instruction.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher collects information about each student's learning and medical needs from a variety of sources and applies it in planning.
No evidence for Knowledge of Students' Special Needs observed.
Ineffective
Teacher displays little or no awareness of the importance of knowing students' special learning or medical needs, and such knowledge may be incomplete or inaccurate.
Developing
Teacher possesses information about students' learning and medical needs.
Effective
Teacher possesses information about students' learning and medical needs and applies this knowledge when planning.
Highly Effective
Teacher possesses information about each student's learning and medical needs, collecting such information from a variety of sources, as appropriate, and applies this knowledge when planning.
1b4
Knowledge of Students' Interests and Cultural Heritage
Uses students' interests and cultural heritage to plan instruction that will engage students. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher recognizes the value of understanding students' interests and demonstrates social awareness of their cultural heritage.
Teacher displays knowledge of cultural heritage and interests for student subgroups.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher recognizes the value of understanding students' interests, demonstrates social awareness of their family and cultural heritage, and displays this knowledge for individual students.
No evidence for Knowledge of Students' Interests and Cultural Heritage observed.
Ineffective
Teacher displays little or no knowledge of students' interests, or social awareness of cultural heritage and does not indicate that such knowledge is valuable.
Developing
Teacher displays little or no knowledge of students' interests, or social awareness of cultural heritage and does not indicate that such knowledge is valuable. (Note: TLF describes Developing and Ineffective identically at this level — the distinction appears in application to planning.)
Effective
Teacher recognizes the value of understanding students' interests, and demonstrates social awareness of their cultural heritage and displays this knowledge for student subgroups.
Highly Effective
Teacher recognizes the value of understanding students' interests, demonstrates social awareness of their family and cultural heritage, and displays this knowledge for individual students.

Component 1c: Establishing Instructional Outcomes

Teaching is goal-directed and designed to achieve certain well-defined purposes. It is through the articulation of instructional outcomes that the teacher describes these purposes. The outcomes should be clear and related to what students are intended to learn as a consequence of instruction. 21st Century outcomes must be included, as students must also learn essential skills such as critical thinking, problem solving, communication, and collaboration. Teacher collaboration strengthens this process.

1c1
Value, Sequence, Alignment, and Clarity
Clear outcomes that are important to the discipline are essential, as is a sequence of learning and alignment to national, state, and/or local standards. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Most or all instructional outcomes are clearly stated in terms of student learning outcomes.
Outcomes represent high expectations and rigor, focused on important grade-level content standards and academic language objectives.
Outcomes reinforce social emotional learning skills and suggest viable methods of assessment.
Outcomes are connected to a sequence of learning and align with 21st Century Skills.
No evidence for Value, Sequence, Alignment, and Clarity of Instructional Outcomes observed.
Ineffective
Instructional outcomes are either not stated, or are stated as activities rather than as student learning. Outcomes represent low expectations and lack of rigor. They do not reflect important learning in the content area, nor do they include language objectives, reinforce SEL skills, or suggest viable methods of assessment.
Developing
Instructional outcomes are unclear or consist of a combination of outcomes and activities, representing moderately high expectations and rigor. Learning expectations are aligned with important grade-level content standards, include language objectives, reinforce SEL skills, and some outcomes suggest viable methods of assessment. There is at least some connection to a sequence of learning and to 21st Century Skills.
Effective
Most instructional outcomes are clearly stated, represent high expectations and rigor, focus on important grade-level content standards and academic language objectives, reinforce SEL skills, and suggest viable methods of assessment. They are connected to a sequence of learning and align with 21st Century Skills.
Highly Effective
All instructional outcomes are clearly stated in terms of student learning outcomes, represent high expectations and rigor, focus on important grade-level content standards and academic language objectives, reinforce SEL skills, and permit viable methods of assessment. They are connected to a sequence of learning both in the discipline and across disciplines and align with 21st Century Skills.
1c2
Suitability for Diverse Learners
Outcomes are planned so that they are appropriate for all students in the class. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Most or all outcomes are suitable for all students in the class.
Outcomes are based on multiple assessments of student learning and social emotional competencies.
At the Highly Effective level: Outcomes are based on comprehensive assessment data and take into account the varying needs of individual students or subgroups.
No evidence for Suitability of Outcomes for Diverse Learners observed.
Ineffective
Instructional outcomes are not suitable for the class or are not based on any assessment of student learning or social emotional competencies.
Developing
Most instructional outcomes are suitable for most of the students in the class based on global assessments of student learning and social emotional competencies.
Effective
Most of the outcomes are suitable for all students in the class and are based on multiple assessments of student learning and social emotional competencies.
Highly Effective
Outcomes are based on a comprehensive assessment of student learning and social emotional competencies and take into account the varying needs of individual students or subgroups.

Component 1d: Designing Coherent Instruction

Teachers translate instructional outcomes into learning experiences for students through the design of instruction. Even in classrooms where students assume considerable responsibility for their learning, teachers must design instruction that is coherent and balanced between careful planning and flexibility in execution. Teachers design instruction that reflects the needs of 21st Century learners and includes opportunities to collaborate, innovate, create, and solve problems using high-level cognitive processes, communication tools, and media.

1d1
Standards-Based Learning Activities
Activities are designed to engage students in cognitively challenging work that is aligned to the standards. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDST Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
All learning activities are relevant, designed to cognitively engage students, and aligned to instructional standards.
Activities reflect awareness of 21st Century Skills.
Learning activities are differentiated as appropriate to meet the needs of diverse student subgroups.
At the Highly Effective level: Activities are differentiated to meet the needs of individual learners — not just subgroups.
No evidence for Standards-Based Learning Activities in planning observed.
EDST Focus Element — Standards-Based Learning Activities (1d1) is a focus element for the EDST evaluation cycle. This element in planning connects directly to 3c1 in delivery — what teachers design in planning (1d1) should be visible in what students do in class (3c1). The distinction between Effective and Highly Effective lies in the level of differentiation: subgroup vs. individual.
Ineffective
Learning activities are not designed to cognitively engage students or they are not aligned with instructional standards.
Developing
Some of the learning activities are designed to cognitively engage students and are aligned to the instructional standards. Some represent a moderate cognitive challenge and there is some differentiation to meet the needs of diverse student subgroups.
Effective
All of the learning activities are relevant, designed to cognitively engage students, are aligned to the instructional standards and represent awareness of 21st Century Skills. Learning activities are differentiated as appropriate to meet the needs of diverse student subgroups.
Highly Effective
All of the learning activities are relevant, designed to cognitively engage students, are aligned to the instructional standards, and reflect 21st Century Skills. Learning activities are differentiated, as appropriate, to meet the needs of individual learners.
1d2
Instructional Materials, Technology, and Resources
Resources in the plan promote high levels of learning and student engagement. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher displays awareness of resources available for classroom use through a variety of sources.
All materials and resources selected are suitable for students, support instructional outcomes, cultural relevance and responsiveness, and are designed to engage students in meaningful learning.
Plan includes appropriate use of technology.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher's knowledge of resources is extensive and students participate in selecting or adapting materials.
No evidence for Instructional Materials, Technology, and Resources planning observed.
Ineffective
Teacher is unaware of resources for classroom use or materials and resources are not suitable for students, do not support the instructional outcomes, do not support cultural relevance and responsiveness, nor do they engage students in meaningful learning.
Developing
Teacher displays some awareness of resources available for classroom use. Some materials, technology, and resources are suitable to students, support the instructional outcomes, cultural relevance and responsiveness, and engage students in meaningful learning.
Effective
Teacher displays awareness of resources available for classroom use through a variety of sources. All materials and resources selected for instruction are suitable for students, support the instructional outcomes, cultural relevance and responsiveness, and are designed to engage students in meaningful learning, including the appropriate use of technology.
Highly Effective
Teacher's knowledge of resources for classroom use is extensive. All materials and resources selected for instruction are suitable for students, support the instructional outcomes, cultural relevance and responsiveness, and are designed to engage students in meaningful learning, including the appropriate use of technology. Students participate in selecting or adapting materials.
1d3
Purposeful Instructional Groups
Groups are purposefully designed to enhance student cognitive engagement. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Instructional groups are purposefully designed to meet students' needs and are based on instructional outcomes.
At the Highly Effective level: Groups promote effective student interaction and offer student choice.
No evidence for Purposeful Instructional Groups in planning observed.
Note: This element in planning (1d3) connects directly to 3c2 in delivery — the purposeful group structures designed in planning should be visible and productive in classroom observation. Evidence for 1d3 comes from lesson plans and pre-observation conferences, not the classroom visit itself.
Ineffective
Instructional groups do not support the instructional outcomes.
Developing
Instructional groups partially support the instructional outcomes.
Effective
Instructional groups are purposefully designed to meet students' needs and are based on instructional outcomes.
Highly Effective
Instructional groups are purposefully designed to meet students' needs and are based on instructional outcomes. Groups promote effective student interaction and offer student choice.
1d4
Lesson and Unit Structure
The lesson/unit is logically designed to allow students sufficient time to achieve the learning outcomes. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
The lesson or unit has a clearly defined, logical structure around which activities are organized.
Structure anticipates student difficulties or confusion.
Progression of activities is even, with reasonable time allocations.
At the Highly Effective level: Structure allows for different pathways according to diverse student needs, anticipates misconceptions, addresses SEL skills, and reflects the needs of 21st Century learners.
No evidence for Lesson and Unit Structure in planning observed.
Ineffective
The lesson or unit has no clearly defined structure, or the structure is chaotic. Activities do not follow an organized progression, and time allocations are unrealistic.
Developing
The lesson or unit has a recognizable structure, although the structure is not uniformly maintained throughout. Progression of activities is uneven; most time allocations are reasonable.
Effective
The lesson or unit has a clearly defined, logical structure around which activities are organized, and which anticipates student difficulties or confusion. Progression of activities is even, with reasonable time allocations.
Highly Effective
The lesson or unit structure is clear and logical, allowing for different pathways according to diverse student needs, anticipating student misconceptions, the social emotional learning skills and the needs of 21st Century learners. The progression of activities is highly coherent with appropriate time allocations.

Component 1e: Designing Student Assessment

Teachers plan and design lessons that reflect an understanding of their disciplines including an understanding of instructional standards, concepts, and principles. Teachers value each discipline and the relationships between disciplines, designing on-going formative assessments that measure student progress. Teachers use multiple measures to demonstrate student growth over time. Teachers should engage in collaborative design and analysis of assessments to strengthen assessment systems and to ensure equitable assessments for students.

1e1
Aligns with Instructional Outcomes
Assessments are purposefully and tightly aligned to the learning outcomes. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
All instructional outcomes are aligned with purposefully selected formal and informal assessments.
Assessment methodologies may have been adapted for student subgroups.
At the Highly Effective level: All formal and informal assessments are purposefully selected and tightly aligned with instructional outcomes in both content and process. Assessment methodologies may have been adapted for individual students.
No evidence for Alignment of Assessments with Instructional Outcomes observed.
Ineffective
Formal and informal assessments are not aligned with instructional outcomes.
Developing
Some of the instructional outcomes are aligned with the formal and informal assessments, but many are not.
Effective
All the instructional outcomes are aligned with purposefully selected formal and informal assessments; assessment methodologies may have been adapted for student subgroups.
Highly Effective
All formal and informal assessments are purposefully selected and tightly aligned with the instructional outcomes, in both content and process. Assessment methodologies may have been adapted for individual students.
1e2
Planning Assessment Criteria
Criteria for the assessments are clear and reflect outcomes being taught. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDST Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher has developed criteria by which levels of student learning will be assessed.
Teacher has planned how criteria will be communicated to students.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher has planned how students will demonstrate their understanding of the criteria. Criteria may include student contributions.
No evidence for Planning of Assessment Criteria observed.
EDST Focus Element — Planning Assessment Criteria (1e2) is a focus element for the EDST evaluation cycle. This element connects directly to 3d1 (Assessment Criteria in delivery) — what the teacher designs in planning should match what students can articulate about how their work will be evaluated. Effective teachers plan not just what the criteria are but how students will come to understand them.
Ineffective
Teacher has not developed criteria by which student learning will be assessed.
Developing
Teacher has developed criteria by which student learning will be assessed.
Effective
Teacher has developed criteria by which levels of student learning will be assessed. Teacher has planned how criteria will be communicated to students.
Highly Effective
Teacher has developed criteria by which levels of student learning will be assessed. Teacher has planned how criteria will be communicated and how students will demonstrate their understanding of the criteria. Criteria may include student contributions.
1e3
Design of Formative Assessments
Formative assessments are purposefully designed to determine student strengths and gaps in content knowledge. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Formative assessments are purposefully designed to determine student strengths and gaps in content knowledge, skills, and/or mastery of standards.
At the Highly Effective level: Formative assessments include both teacher and student use of the assessment information.
No evidence for Design of Formative Assessments observed.
Ineffective
Teacher has no plan to incorporate formative assessment in the lesson or unit.
Developing
Formative assessments are designed to highlight student strengths and gaps related to some content, skills, or standards.
Effective
Formative assessments are purposefully designed to determine student strengths and gaps in content knowledge, skills, and/or mastery of standards.
Highly Effective
Formative assessments are purposefully designed to determine student strengths and gaps in content knowledge, skills, and mastery of standards, and includes student as well as teacher use of the assessment information.
1e4
Analysis and Use of Assessment Data for Planning
Teacher uses assessment data to determine next steps in instruction. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher consistently uses multiple measures of growth for learners including both formative and summative data.
Teacher analyzes and uses assessment data to plan future instruction for subgroups of learners.
Plan includes re-teaching and reassessment if necessary.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher disaggregates and analyzes assessment data and uses information to plan future instruction for individuals, including re-teaching and reassessment if necessary.
No evidence for Analysis and Use of Assessment Data for Planning observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Analysis and Use of Assessment Data for Planning (1e4) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. This element connects directly to the data-driven instruction work at the heart of Region South's instructional leadership model — it asks whether teachers are using student performance data (including iReady, SBA, and classroom assessments) to actually change what they plan next.
Ineffective
Teacher rarely and/or ineffectively uses multiple measures of growth for learners including formative and summative data that may include learners' work, to demonstrate participant learning. Teacher does not analyze or use assessment data to design future instruction.
Developing
Teacher inconsistently uses multiple measures of growth for learners including both formative and summative data that include learners' work to demonstrate participant learning. Teacher analyzes and uses some assessment data to plan for future instruction for the class/group as a whole.
Effective
Teacher consistently uses multiple measures of growth for learners including both formative and summative data that include learners' work to demonstrate participant learning. Teacher analyzes and uses assessment data to plan future instruction for subgroups of learners, including re-teaching and reassessment if necessary.
Highly Effective
Teacher consistently uses multiple measures of growth for learners including both formative and summative data including learners' work to demonstrate a high level of participant learning. Teacher disaggregates and analyzes assessment data and uses information to plan future instruction for individuals, including re-teaching and reassessment if necessary.
