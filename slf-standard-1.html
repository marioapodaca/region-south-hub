SLF Standard 1: Leadership and Professional Growth

School leaders have a responsibility to be the "chief learner" at a school site — modeling reflective practice, ethical decision-making, professional growth, and other learning-centered leadership behaviors. Standard 1 describes how the principal shows up as a professional: their integrity, how they allocate their time, how they build their observation skills, how they reflect and grow from professional development, how they lead through ambiguity, and how they demonstrate cultural proficiency. During school site visits, Standard 1 evidence is most visible in how the principal organizes the visit, what they notice in classrooms, and how they prepare for the post-visit debrief.

Component 1A: Models Learning-Centered Leadership Behaviors

This is the only component in Standard 1. All seven elements describe different dimensions of how a principal models learning-centered leadership — from ethics and time management to cultural proficiency and leading through ambiguity. Evidence is gathered through observation of the principal in action, professional conversations, and artifacts such as schedules, MyPGS records, and PD documentation.

1A.1
Demonstrates Personal and Professional Ethics, Integrity, and Judgment
The principal models ethical behavior and sound professional judgment in all interactions within and outside the school.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Consistently models ethical behavior and integrity in interactions within and outside the school.
Administers school policies with integrity, including the use of school funds and resources.
Exhibits professionalism when interacting with all members of the school community.
Most stakeholders recognize the leader as one who exhibits integrity, ethical behavior, and sound professional judgment when making decisions or taking action.
No evidence for Ethical Conduct, Integrity, and Judgment observed.
Examples of Evidence
Surveys of staff, students, and parents Observation of interactions with staff, parents, students, and community Documentation of Student Body funds and P-card expenditures Resource and facility use records
Ineffective
Violates the tenets of integrity or ethical behavior within or outside of the school, or knowingly or carelessly violates administration of school policies, including the misuse of school funds or resources. Stakeholders articulate concerns regarding the leader's behavior, integrity, or professional judgment.
Developing
Inconsistently demonstrates ethical behavior and integrity in interactions within or outside of the school; generally administers school policies with integrity. Stakeholders may question the leader's behavior, integrity, or professional judgment when making decisions or taking action.
Effective
Consistently models ethical behavior and integrity in interactions within and outside the school and administers school policies with integrity; exhibits professionalism when interacting with all members of the school community. Most stakeholders recognize the leader as one who exhibits integrity, ethical behavior, and sound professional judgment.
Highly Effective
Continuously demonstrates and transparently models ethical behavior and integrity in all interactions and positively influences the culture of professionalism among all members of the school community. Stakeholders recognize the leader as one who models integrity and ethical behavior and uses sound professional judgment.
1A.2
Manages Time and Prioritizes High-Leverage Activities
The principal identifies high-leverage activities, organizes their schedule strategically, and delegates responsibilities to ensure that instruction receives adequate attention and oversight.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Identifies high-leverage activities and proactively organizes schedule to ensure they receive adequate attention.
Strategically delegates responsibilities so that instructional leadership receives consistent focus.
High-leverage activities and school priority areas — especially instruction — are the focus of administrator oversight.
No evidence for Managing Time and Prioritizing High-Leverage Activities observed.
Examples of Evidence
Weekly or monthly schedule of completed observations and conferences To-do list and list of delegated responsibilities Action plans and ILT/faculty meeting agendas Completion of district/region priority tasks on time (EL accountability, EDST process, IEPs)
Ineffective
Does not identify high-leverage activities; fails to organize schedule, or focuses primarily on low-priority activities. Does not appropriately delegate responsibilities; consequently, high-leverage activities and school priority areas (e.g., instruction) receive inadequate or no attention.
Developing
Identifies high-leverage activities; organizes schedule and inconsistently delegates responsibilities so that high-leverage activities and school priority areas (e.g., instruction) receive limited attention.
Effective
Identifies high-leverage activities; proactively organizes schedule and strategically delegates responsibilities to ensure that high-leverage activities and school priority areas (e.g., instruction) receive adequate attention and are the focus of administrator oversight.
Highly Effective
Systematically identifies high-leverage activities for self and other staff; proactively organizes schedule and strategically delegates responsibilities to ensure high-leverage activities are the ongoing focus of administrator attention. Collaborates with staff to ensure that their time is aligned with the highest-leverage activities.
1A.3
Builds Observation and Coaching Skills to Improve Instruction
The principal regularly practices and builds skills in observation note-taking, alignment of evidence to TLF elements, rating accuracy, and coaching individual teachers.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Regularly initiates opportunities to practice and build skills in observation note-taking and alignment of evidence to elements of the Teaching and Learning Framework.
Demonstrates accuracy in rating teacher practice.
Uses evidence from observations to coach individual teachers.
Is certified in observation skills.
No evidence for Building Observation and Coaching Skills observed.
Examples of Evidence
Paired or peer/team observations for calibration Change in quality/quantity of observation notes in MyPGS Observation of coaching conference by supervisor Documentation from instructional rounds or team walk-throughs Observer Training Certification Results of ongoing observer calibrations
Ineffective
Rarely participates in opportunities to improve skills in observation note-taking, alignment of evidence to elements of the TLF, rating accuracy, or coaching individual teachers; is not yet preliminarily certified in observation skills or accuracy in rating teacher practice.
Developing
Occasionally initiates opportunities to practice and build skills in observation note-taking, alignment of evidence to elements of the TLF, rating accuracy, or coaching individual teachers; is preliminarily certified in observation skills or demonstrates limited accuracy in rating teacher practice.
Effective
Regularly initiates opportunities to practice and build skills in observation note-taking, alignment of evidence to elements of the TLF, rating accuracy, and using evidence to coach individual teachers; is certified in observation skills and demonstrates accuracy in rating teacher practice.
Highly Effective
Systematically initiates opportunities for self and instructional staff to practice and build skills in observation note-taking, alignment of evidence to TLF elements, rating accuracy, and using evidence to provide feedback and coaching; is certified (or higher) in observation skills and is consistently accurate in rating teacher practice.
1A.4
Uses Reflection to Inform Future Decisions and Actions
The principal regularly and accurately reflects on the results of decisions made or actions taken, and uses reflection to inform future decisions or actions.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Regularly and accurately uses criteria and data to reflect on the results of decisions made or actions taken.
Uses reflection to inform future decisions or actions.
Models reflective practice in relation to the school's vision and goals.
Professional growth and changes in school practices can be attributed to the school leader's reflections.
No evidence for Using Reflection to Inform Future Decisions and Actions observed.
The post-visit debrief is a direct window into 1A.4 — how the principal reflects on what they observed, what they would do differently in their coaching approach, and how they connect classroom evidence to school-wide instructional priorities.
Examples of Evidence
MyPGS EDSSL Self-Assessment completed MyPGS Initial Growth Plan Notes from Growth Planning, Midyear, and End-of-Year Conferences PD plans that include staff reflection opportunities School action plans demonstrating improvement based upon reflection ILT meeting notes showing team use of student or staff reflection
Ineffective
Rarely engages in reflective practices, or limited reflections do not result in a change in practice or professional growth.
Developing
Occasionally or inaccurately reflects on the results of decisions made or actions taken, and uses general impressions to inform future decisions or actions; occasionally encourages staff or student reflection. Inconsistent or inaccurate reflection results in minimal changes in practice or professional growth.
Effective
Regularly and accurately uses criteria and data to reflect on the results of decisions made or actions taken; uses reflection to inform future decisions or actions; models reflective practice in relation to the school's vision and goals. Professional growth and changes in school practices can be attributed to the school leader's reflections.
Highly Effective
Systematically and accurately uses criteria and data to reflect on the results of decisions made or actions taken over time, and systematically uses reflection to inform future decisions or actions; establishes a culture of regular opportunities for staff and student reflection and self-assessment in relation to the school's vision and goals. The reflective culture of the school community contributes to a school-wide climate of ongoing or sustained growth and improvement of practice.
1A.5
Participates in, and Implements New Learning from, Professional Development
The principal analyzes data to determine professional development needs, actively seeks out PD opportunities, implements new learning, and tracks the degree to which practice has changed and the school has been impacted.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Analyzes data to determine professional development needs.
Actively and regularly seeks out opportunities for professional development to enhance content knowledge or leadership skills.
Implements current, research-based learning from professional development.
Tracks the degree to which practice has changed or the school has been impacted.
No evidence for Participation in and Implementation of New Learning from Professional Development observed.
Examples of Evidence
Implementation of action steps from Initial Growth Plan in MyPGS Observation of change in school leader's leadership and leadership team Transcripts of professional coursework Regular attendance and involvement in administrator PD Demonstrates cultural proficiency and knowledge of SEL resulting from administrator PD
Ineffective
Ignores or dismisses the use of data when determining professional development needs. Rarely participates in professional development, or does not implement current, research-based learning from professional development.
Developing
Uses some data analysis to determine professional development needs. Occasionally participates in professional development to enhance content knowledge or leadership skills. Attempts to implement current, research-based learning from professional development, but with limited impact.
Effective
Analyzes data to determine professional development needs. Actively and regularly seeks out opportunities for professional development to enhance content knowledge or leadership skills. Implements current, research-based learning from professional development and tracks the degree to which practice has changed or the school has been impacted.
Highly Effective
Systematically analyzes data to determine professional development needs. Actively and regularly seeks out opportunities for professional development to enhance content knowledge and leadership skills. Works with peers or leadership team to implement current, research-based learning from professional development and tracks the degree to which practices have changed and the school has been impacted.
1A.6
Leads in Situations of Ambiguity or Complexity
The principal provides consistent leadership, guided by policy when appropriate, even when confronted with unfamiliar, volatile, or uncertain situations.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Provides consistent leadership guided by policy when confronted with unfamiliar, volatile, or uncertain situations.
Reacts thoughtfully to contradictory or incomplete information, seeking more information when possible.
Leadership leads to broader understanding and acceptance of change, and ultimately to positive outcomes.
No evidence for Leading in Situations of Ambiguity or Complexity observed.
Examples of Evidence
Observation of school leader's leadership in uncomfortable or challenging conversations School leader articulates rationale for change decisions Communications to and between school community members Implementation timeline includes stakeholder feedback on proposed changes IEP and student placement discussions
Ineffective
Is uncertain regarding how to proceed or hesitant to lead when confronted with unfamiliar, volatile, or uncertain situations. Reacts emotionally or without thinking of consequences, and is unable to change course or manage the elements of change when information is contradictory.
Developing
Provides inconsistent leadership when confronted with unfamiliar, volatile, or uncertain situations. Reacts to incomplete or contradictory information when managing change, resulting in confusion or minimally-effective outcomes.
Effective
Provides consistent leadership, guided by policy when appropriate, even when confronted with unfamiliar, volatile, or uncertain situations. Reacts thoughtfully to contradictory or incomplete information, seeking more information when possible. This leads to broader understanding and acceptance of the change, and ultimately to positive outcomes.
Highly Effective
Provides consistent leadership, guided by policy when appropriate, and builds leadership team capacity even when confronted with unfamiliar, volatile, and uncertain situations. Works with leadership team members to clarify and expand contradictory or incomplete information, so that change is well-managed and understood by individuals, challenges are viewed as learning opportunities, and positive outcomes are attained and accepted.
1A.7
Demonstrates Cultural Proficiency and Knowledge of the LAUSD Competencies of Social-Emotional Learning
The principal initiates learning about other cultures, seeks out best practices to enhance social-emotional learning, and models culturally-proficient practices and SEL competencies in interactions with adults and students.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Initiates learning about other cultures and seeks out best practices to enhance social-emotional learning.
Models culturally-proficient practices and SEL competencies in interactions with adults and students.
Uses cultural knowledge and information on best practices surrounding SEL, including trauma-informed practices, to guide school policies and decisions.
No evidence for Cultural Proficiency and Knowledge of SEL Competencies observed.
Examples of Evidence · LAUSD SEL Competencies: Growth Mindset · Self-Efficacy · Self-Management · Social Awareness
School leader demonstrates cultural proficiency resulting from administrator PD School leader models flexibility in instructional style and validation of multiple cultures Observation of school leader/parent interaction Implementation of trauma-informed practices that promote resilience
Ineffective
Rarely participates in opportunities to learn about other cultures or best practices to enhance SEL; may demonstrate cultural incapacity or cultural blindness in interactions with adults or students; does not use cultural knowledge, information on SEL best practices, or trauma-informed practices to guide school policies or decisions.
Developing
Occasionally participates in opportunities to learn about other cultures or best practices to enhance SEL; inconsistently models culturally-competent practices and SEL competencies in interactions with adults and students; uses limited cultural knowledge or information on SEL best practices, occasionally including trauma-informed practices, to guide school policies and decisions.
Effective
Initiates learning about other cultures and seeks out best practices to enhance SEL; models culturally-proficient practices and SEL competencies in interactions with adults and students; uses cultural knowledge and information on SEL best practices, including trauma-informed practices, to guide school policies and decisions.
Highly Effective
Actively and regularly initiates learning about other cultures and seeks out best practices to enhance SEL; consistently models and teaches culturally-proficient practices and SEL competencies in interactions with adults and students; collaboratively uses cultural knowledge and information on SEL best practices, including trauma-informed practices, to guide school policies and decisions.
