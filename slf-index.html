School Leadership Framework

The LAUSD School Leadership Framework v14 describes the actions leaders take to create systems, structures, and school culture that improve student learning and teacher effectiveness. Region South uses the SLF as the foundation for principal coaching, observation practice, formal evaluation, and instructional leadership and principal coaching conversations.

How the SLF Is Used in Principal Evaluation and Supervision

The SLF governs how principals are evaluated, coached, and developed across all six standards. Each standard describes a dimension of effective leadership — from managing change and building culture to engaging families and maintaining operational systems. All six standards are assessed over the course of the evaluation cycle through multiple evidence sources: formal observation of practice, professional conversations, artifacts, and data.

Three elements are designated as unified focus elements — assessed on every principal's Observation of Practice Rating regardless of which cooperatively-established elements are selected:

3A.2Ensures high-quality professional development
3C.1Conducts formal and informal observations
3C.2Provides actionable feedback to staff

Up to 2 additional cooperatively-established elements are selected each cycle based on the principal's growth goals and school context.

About the SLF

The LAUSD School Leadership Framework v14 is comprised of six standards, each divided into components and elements — behaviors or actions in relation to particular leadership skills. The SLF aligns with the 2015 Professional Standards for Educational Leaders (PSELs) and the 2014 California Professional Standards for Educational Leaders (CPSELs).

The Observation of Practice Rating uses 3 unified focus elements plus up to 2 cooperatively-established elements per evaluation cycle. The three unified focus elements across all principal evaluations are: 3A.2 (Ensures high-quality professional development), 3C.1 (Conducts formal and informal observations), and 3C.2 (Provides actionable feedback to staff).

SLF Levels of Performance
Ineffective
Demonstrates limited knowledge of the SLF and does not implement the standards. Practice and outcomes require immediate attention, intervention, and coaching from supervisors.
Developing
Aware of effective leadership practices, but does not consistently or effectively implement them. May demonstrate appropriate effort but cannot yet show evidence of impact on school community.
Effective
Consistently implements effective leadership practices across the standards. Members of the school community demonstrate effectiveness and growth that derive in part from the school's leadership.
Highly Effective
Demonstrates extraordinary leadership — sustained, consistent growth for nearly all school community members. Builds others' capacity to assume leadership roles within the school and beyond.
