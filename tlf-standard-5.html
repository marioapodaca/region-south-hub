Standard 5: Professional Growth

Standard 5 addresses the ongoing learning cycle that connects observation evidence to teacher development. It encompasses two components: reflecting on practice (5a) and participating in a professional community (5b). Reflecting on teaching is the mark of a true professional — the belief that teaching can never be perfect, yet it can be continually improved. Standard 5 is most visible in the post-observation conference, the growth planning process, and a teacher's active engagement in professional learning communities. It closes the loop between what is observed in Standards 2 and 3 and what the teacher does as a result.

How Standard 5 Connects to the Full TLF Cycle

Standard 5 is the reflective engine of the TLF. What a teacher planned (Standard 1) is observed in the classroom (Standards 2 and 3) and documented in records and professional practice (Standard 4). Standard 5 asks: what does the teacher do with that experience? Accurate reflection (5a1) leads to changed instruction (5a2), which drives professional development choices (5a3) and new learning (5a4). Participation in professional community (5b) multiplies that growth across colleagues and classrooms. The post-observation conference is the primary venue where Standard 5 evidence is generated.

Component 5a: Reflecting on Practice

Reflecting on teaching is the mark of a true professional. The importance of reflection on practice is governed by the belief that teaching can never be perfect, yet it can be continually improved. With practice and experience in reflection, teachers become more discerning and can evaluate both their successes and errors. Reflective practice enhances both teaching and learning. Skilled reflection is characterized by accuracy, specificity, and ability to use the analysis of their reflection in future teaching as well as the ability to consider multiple perspectives. Other evidence of reflection includes videotaping, journaling, action research, and student work analysis.

5a1
Accurate Reflection
The teacher can analyze practice and student work to determine what went well in the lesson. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher uses criteria to assess a lesson's effectiveness and the extent to which it achieved its instructional goals.
Teacher cites specific evidence from the lesson to support the judgment.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher uses specific criteria to assess effectiveness, cites specific examples from the lesson, and provides rationales for instructional choices.
No evidence for Accurate Reflection on Practice observed.
Post-observation conference indicator: Listen for whether the teacher's self-assessment is grounded in student evidence — what students did and said — rather than general impressions about the lesson. An Ineffective reflector misjudges the lesson's success. An Effective reflector names specific evidence. A Highly Effective reflector can explain why they made each instructional decision and what they would do differently.
Ineffective
Teacher misjudges the success of a lesson.
Developing
Teacher has a general impression of a lesson's effectiveness and uses that impression to determine the extent to which instructional goals were met.
Effective
Teacher uses criteria to assess a lesson's effectiveness and the extent to which it achieved its instructional goals; the teacher cites evidence to support the judgment.
Highly Effective
Teacher uses specific criteria to assess a lesson's effectiveness and the extent to which it achieved its instructional goals. The teacher cites specific examples from the lesson to support the judgment and provides rationales for instructional choices.
5a2
Use of Reflection to Inform Future Instruction
The teacher uses reflection to inform future lessons. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDST + EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher describes specific next steps to build on student learning, to address student misunderstandings, or to enhance a teaching practice that was tried.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher offers intentional teaching practices as next steps, provides a rationale for instructional options, and describes the anticipated impact on learning.
No evidence for Use of Reflection to Inform Future Instruction observed.
EDST Focus Element and EDSNCT Focus Element — Use of Reflection to Inform Future Instruction (5a2) is a unified focus element for both evaluation cycles. This is the most critical element in Standard 5 — it is the bridge between what was observed and what will change. The distinction between Developing and Effective is specificity: general suggestions vs. concrete next steps. The distinction between Effective and Highly Effective is intentionality: next steps vs. next steps with rationale and anticipated impact on student learning.
Ineffective
Teacher has no suggestions for what could be improved for next steps in subsequent lessons.
Developing
Teacher offers general suggestions for what could be improved to build on student learning, to address student misunderstandings, or to enhance a teaching practice that was tried.
Effective
Teacher describes specific next steps to build on student learning, to address student misunderstandings, or to enhance a teaching practice that was tried.
Highly Effective
Teacher offers intentional teaching practices to build on student learning, to address student misunderstandings, or to enhance a teaching practice that was tried. The teacher provides a rationale for instructional options, describing anticipated impact on learning.
5a3
Selection of Professional Development Based on Reflection and Data
The teacher uses reflection and various forms of data to determine professional development needs. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher uses information from self and peer analysis, along with data on student achievement, to determine professional development needs.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher continually uses information from self and peer analysis, along with data on student achievement, to determine and prioritize professional development needs.
No evidence for Data-Driven Selection of Professional Development observed.
Connection to Region South's instructional leadership model: This element directly links teacher reflection to the iReady, SBA, and formative assessment data that drives Region South's instructional focus. An Effective teacher uses student performance data — not just personal preference or administrative assignment — to decide where to invest in their own growth.
Ineffective
Teacher does not use information from self and peer analysis, or data on student achievement to determine professional development needs.
Developing
Teacher uses information from self and peer analysis, or data on student achievement to determine professional development needs.
Effective
Teacher uses information from self and peer analysis, along with data on student achievement, to determine professional development needs.
Highly Effective
Teacher continually uses information from self and peer analysis, along with data on student achievement, to determine and prioritize professional development needs.
5a4
Implementation of New Learning from Professional Development
The teacher implements new learning into the classroom setting and monitors progress towards deep implementation. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher seeks out opportunities for professional development to enhance content knowledge and pedagogical skill.
Teacher implements new learning from professional development.
Teacher tracks the degree to which student achievement is positively impacted by the new practice.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher tracks impact on individual student achievement and works with peers to deepen implementation.
No evidence for Implementation of New Learning from Professional Development observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Implementation of New Learning from Professional Development (5a4) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. This element asks the most important question about professional development: did it change practice? An Effective teacher doesn't just attend PD — they implement what they learned and monitor whether it moved student achievement. A Highly Effective teacher extends that work with peers to scale implementation across classrooms.
Ineffective
Teacher engages in no professional development activities to enhance knowledge or skill, or does not implement new learning in the classroom.
Developing
Teacher participates in professional activities to a limited extent. Teacher attempts to implement new learning from professional development, with limited success.
Effective
Teacher seeks out opportunities for professional development to enhance content knowledge and pedagogical skill. Teacher implements new learning from professional development and tracks the degree to which student achievement is positively impacted.
Highly Effective
Teacher seeks out opportunities for professional development to enhance content knowledge and pedagogical skill. Teacher implements new learning from professional development and tracks the degree to which individual student achievement is positively impacted. Teacher works with peers to deepen implementation.

Component 5b: Participating in a Professional Community

Participation in a professional community requires active involvement, the promotion of a culture of collaboration and inquiry that improves the culture of teaching and learning. Relationships with colleagues are an important aspect of creating a culture where expertise, materials, insights, and experiences are shared. The goal of the professional community is improved teaching and learning — not compliance with meeting attendance requirements.

5b1
Relationships with Colleagues
The teacher supports and cooperates with colleagues in order to promote a professional school culture. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Relationships with colleagues are supportive and cooperative.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher takes initiative in promoting positive relationships among colleagues — not just maintaining them.
No evidence for Positive Relationships with Colleagues observed.
Ineffective
Teacher's relationships with colleagues are often negative or disruptive.
Developing
Teacher maintains cordial relationships with some colleagues.
Effective
Relationships with colleagues are supportive and cooperative.
Highly Effective
Relationships with colleagues are supportive and cooperative. Teacher takes initiative in promoting positive relationships among colleagues.
5b2
Promotes a Culture of Professional Inquiry and Collaboration
The teacher promotes a culture of inquiry for the purpose of improving teaching and learning and collaborates with colleagues to do so. Evidence source: Professional Conversation (PC), Artifacts (A).
EDSNCT Focus Element
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Teacher actively participates in department, grade-level, school-wide, or district-sponsored professional development meetings.
Teacher promotes a culture of professional inquiry and collaboration by actively participating in professional learning communities, peer observations, lesson study, teaming, or other inquiry models with colleagues.
At the Highly Effective level: Teacher actively participates in and contributes to the design or facilitation of PD meetings. Teacher initiates or takes a leadership role in PLCs, lesson study, teaming, or other inquiry models with colleagues.
No evidence for Promoting a Culture of Professional Inquiry and Collaboration observed.
EDSNCT Focus Element — Promotes a Culture of Professional Inquiry and Collaboration (5b2) is a focus element for the EDSNCT evaluation cycle. Note: For EDST, 5b2 may also be collected as evidence for Contributions to School Community (CSC). The critical distinction between Effective and Highly Effective is leadership: an Effective teacher actively participates in inquiry structures; a Highly Effective teacher designs, facilitates, or leads them. Attendance alone — even enthusiastic attendance — does not reach Effective.
Ineffective
Teacher resists attendance at required department, grade-level, school-wide, or district-sponsored professional development meetings.
Developing
Teacher attends only required department, grade-level, school-wide, or district-sponsored professional development meetings.
Effective
Teacher actively participates in department, grade-level, school-wide, or district-sponsored professional development meetings. Teacher promotes a culture of professional inquiry and collaboration by actively participating in professional learning communities, peer observations, lesson study, teaming, or other inquiry models with colleagues.
Highly Effective
Teacher actively participates in and contributes to the design or facilitation of department, grade-level, school-wide, or district-sponsored professional development meetings. Teacher initiates or takes a leadership role in professional learning communities, lesson study, teaming, or other inquiry models with colleagues.
