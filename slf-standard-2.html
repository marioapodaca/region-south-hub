SLF Standard 2: Change Management

Standard 2 addresses how principals help create, maintain, and communicate a shared vision around high expectations for student learning — and how they build the culture, urgency, and structures needed to sustain meaningful improvement. It consists of three components: 2A (Leads and manages change), 2B (Develops or maintains a shared vision around high expectations for student learning), and 2C (Creates or maintains a culture of continuous improvement).

Standard 2 is most visible over time — in how a principal communicates about student learning, how the school community understands and owns the school's direction, and whether improvement is incremental and data-driven or episodic and reactive. During school site visits, elements of 2C are directly observable: does the school's data infrastructure support the continuous improvement cycle, and can teachers and staff articulate school-wide goals and their progress toward them?

Component 2A: Leads and Manages Change

This component addresses how principals build shared responsibility for all students, communicate urgency for change, and build staff capacity to adapt to and sustain improvement. Effective change management is not compliance — it is the work of helping a school community understand why change must happen, believe that it can, and develop the skills to make it real and lasting.

2A.1
Promotes a Shared Sense of Responsibility for All Students
Consistently communicates and promotes the school community's responsibility for all students — not just those in their own classrooms. Most staff demonstrate a commitment to and responsibility for all students.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Consistently communicates and promotes the school community's responsibility for all students.
Most staff demonstrate a commitment to and responsibility for all students, not just those in their own classrooms.
At the Highly Effective level: Systematically communicates by multiple means; nearly all staff demonstrate and articulate a commitment to and responsibility for all students.
No evidence for Promoting a Shared Sense of Responsibility for All Students observed.
Site visit indicator: How staff talk about students during leadership visits is evidence for 2A.1. Teachers who say "our students" and reference school-wide data alongside their own classroom data are demonstrating the culture this element describes. Teachers who speak only of their own students or deflect responsibility for struggling learners signal that the principal has not yet established shared accountability norms.
Examples of Evidence
Observation of informal and school-wide settings where teachers demonstrate a commitment to students who are not their own
Written and oral communication that includes references to "shared responsibility"
Language of "we" and "our students" used consistently by stakeholders
Agenda items with notes showing a discussion or emphasis on "shared responsibility"
Protocols established for collaborative student intervention and inclusion across classrooms and teachers
Evidence of linguistically and culturally-responsive practices implemented throughout the school
Ineffective
Does not communicate or promote the school community's responsibility for all students; few staff demonstrate a responsibility for students who are not in their own classrooms.
Developing
Inconsistently communicates or promotes the school community's responsibility for all students; some staff demonstrate a commitment to and responsibility for all students, not just those in their own classrooms.
Effective
Consistently communicates and promotes the school community's responsibility for all students; most staff demonstrate a commitment to and responsibility for all students, not just those in their own classrooms.
Highly Effective
Systematically communicates and promotes by multiple means the school community's responsibility for all students; nearly all staff demonstrate and articulate a commitment to and responsibility for all students, not just those in their own classrooms.
2A.2
Communicates a Sense of Urgency for Sustainable Change
Consistently challenges current practices that are unlikely to increase student learning; makes a compelling case for change based on moral imperatives or data, communicating a sense of urgency around high-leverage activities.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Consistently challenges current practices that are unlikely to increase student learning.
Makes a compelling case for change based on moral imperatives or data, communicating a sense of urgency around high-leverage activities.
Demonstrates that some change can occur within one school year, so that staff and students see that change is possible.
At the Highly Effective level: Makes a persuasive case based on moral imperatives AND data; works with others in the school community to demonstrate that changes can occur within one school year so that stakeholders recognize that change has occurred.
No evidence for Communicating a Sense of Urgency for Sustainable Change observed.
Note on moral imperative: The SLF distinguishes between making a case for change based on compliance or the law (Developing), moral imperatives (Effective), and moral imperatives plus data together (Highly Effective). A principal who says "we have to do this because district policy requires it" is demonstrating Developing. A principal who says "our students deserve better and here is what the data shows about where we are falling short" is demonstrating Effective or Highly Effective practice.
Examples of Evidence
Memos to staff and notes from school meetings where school leader articulates why and how change must occur
Public articulation of reasons why school must change (e.g., graduation rates, CAASPP results, EL redesignation rates, inclusion)
School leader models a specific change in their own practice
Surveys indicate a change from previous years in staff beliefs or practices
Agreed-upon written action plans; use of a change management framework
School leader involves leadership team/staff in communicating need for change; demonstrates successful practices
Ineffective
Does not recognize or acknowledge a case for change; or makes changes without attempting to prepare stakeholders for the change or attending to their concerns; may resist changes that other stakeholders propose to benefit student learning.
Developing
Inconsistently challenges current practices that are unlikely to increase student learning; communicates a sense of urgency around high-leverage priorities or activities and demonstrates that change can occur quickly, but with limited impact; makes a case for change based on compliance or the law.
Effective
Consistently challenges current practices that are unlikely to increase student learning; makes a compelling case for change based on either moral imperatives or data, communicating a sense of urgency around high-leverage activities; demonstrates that some change can occur within one school year, so that staff and students see that change is possible.
Highly Effective
Systematically challenges current practices that are unlikely to increase student learning; makes a persuasive case for change based on moral imperatives and data, communicating a sense of urgency and shared responsibility around high-leverage priorities; works with others in the school community to demonstrate that changes can occur within one school year, so that stakeholders recognize that change has occurred.
2A.3
Builds Capacity of Staff to Adapt to and Manage Change
Builds capacity of staff to determine what needs to change based on data or moral imperatives; most staff articulate the need for change, demonstrate a commitment to incremental and relentless progress, and identify specific evidence of school-wide changes.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Builds capacity of staff to determine what needs to change based on data or moral imperatives.
Most staff articulate the need for change, demonstrate a commitment to incremental and relentless progress, and identify specific evidence of school-wide changes.
At the Highly Effective level: Builds capacity of all staff to determine what needs to be changed; nearly all staff articulate the need for change and demonstrate a commitment to a cycle of continuous improvement; evidence of systemic school-wide changes is identifiable.
No evidence for Building Capacity of Staff to Adapt to and Manage Change observed.
Examples of Evidence
Use of protocols, facilitators, and coaches to teach staff how to analyze data and determine data-based changes
Staff articulate that they feel supported when trying to implement changes
Staff implement changes on their own and determine whether changes are effective
Teacher reflection evidence; initial planning sheets
Monitoring section of SPSA or DVR
Action plans and strategic documents providing evidence of a commitment to school-wide change
Celebrations and affirmation of positive change
Ineffective
Does not support staff in reflecting on what needs to change, or does not use data or moral imperatives to make the case for change. Few staff articulate the need for change, demonstrate a commitment to making changes, or identify evidence of change.
Developing
Supports staff in reflecting on what needs to change, based on data or moral imperatives. Some staff articulate the need for change, demonstrate a commitment to incremental progress, and identify specific evidence of change.
Effective
Builds capacity of staff to determine what needs to change based on data or moral imperatives. Most staff articulate the need for change, demonstrate a commitment to incremental and relentless progress, and identify specific evidence of school-wide changes.
Highly Effective
Builds capacity of all staff to determine what needs to be changed based on data and moral imperatives. Nearly all staff articulate the need for change, demonstrate a commitment to moving forward incrementally and relentlessly in a cycle of continuous improvement, and identify specific evidence of systemic school-wide changes.

Component 2B: Develops or Maintains a Shared Vision Around High Expectations for Student Learning

This component addresses how principals develop, communicate, and sustain a shared vision of high expectations for all learners. The vision must be more than a posted statement — it must be co-created with stakeholders, grounded in values and beliefs, and evident in the day-to-day decisions and conversations of the school community. An effective principal's school vision is something staff, students, and families can articulate and own.

2B.1
Develops, Implements, and Revises a Shared Vision of High Expectations
With most stakeholder groups, facilitates the development, review, or revision of a shared vision based on values and beliefs of high expectations for all learners, including college and career readiness; multiple data points are used to inform or revise the vision.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
With most stakeholder groups, facilitates the development, review, or revision of a shared vision based on values and beliefs of high expectations for all learners, including college and career readiness.
Multiple data points are used to inform or revise the vision.
At the Highly Effective level: With representation from all stakeholders, co-creates or revises a shared vision based on thoroughly-explored values and beliefs. Multiple data points are used to inform or revise the vision.
No evidence for Developing, Implementing, and Revising a Shared Vision of High Expectations observed.
Examples of Evidence
Vision or mission statement — developed collaboratively, not independently
Notes from meetings where vision or mission statements are crafted, reviewed, and revised
Vision or mission statements on school website
Reference to the school vision in action plans or goal-related documents
Data showing how the vision is being attained
LRE Report; promotes dual enrollment; data for dual enrollment
Ineffective
Independently develops, reviews, or revises a vision of high expectations for learners with no input from stakeholders, or does not develop a school vision. Values and beliefs are not explored. Data are not used to inform or revise the vision.
Developing
With minimal input from stakeholders, develops, reviews, or revises a vision of high expectations for all learners. Values and beliefs are minimally explored. Limited data points are used to inform or revise the vision.
Effective
With most stakeholder groups, facilitates the development, review, or revision of a shared vision based on values and beliefs of high expectations for all learners, including college and career readiness. Multiple data points are used to inform or revise the vision.
Highly Effective
With representation from all stakeholders, co-creates, reviews, or revises a shared vision based on thoroughly-explored values and beliefs of high expectations for all learners, including college and career readiness. Multiple data points are used to inform or revise the vision.
2B.2
Communicates Shared Vision to Stakeholders
The vision is communicated regularly to all stakeholders orally and through actions and documents; staff, students, and parents are able to articulate the vision or its implications and relationship to school goals.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
The vision is communicated regularly to all stakeholders orally and through actions and documents.
Staff, students, and parents are able to articulate the vision or its implications and relationship to school goals.
At the Highly Effective level: The vision is communicated systematically and clearly to and by nearly all stakeholders. Staff, students, and parents articulate ownership of, and a belief in, the school's vision and related goals.
No evidence for Communicating Shared Vision to Stakeholders observed.
Site visit indicator: Ask any staff member during a leadership visit: "What is this school working toward this year?" If most staff can articulate a coherent, shared answer connected to student data and improvement goals, that is strong evidence for 2B.2. If answers vary widely or staff defer to a posted statement without being able to explain it, that signals Developing practice.
Examples of Evidence
Written and oral communications — all include reference to vision or mission
Observation of stakeholders articulating their understanding of the school's vision or mission
Reference to the school vision or mission in action plans or goal-related documents
Data showing how the vision is being attained
Highlighting the vision or mission when reviewing or revisiting school priorities, during PD or parent outreach
Discussion of school website development that includes posting of vision and mission
Ineffective
The vision is rarely communicated orally or in writing to stakeholders. Staff, students, and parents are unaware of the school's vision, or actively disagree with the vision.
Developing
The vision is occasionally communicated orally or in writing to stakeholders. Staff, students, or parents may not be able to articulate what the vision is.
Effective
The vision is communicated regularly to all stakeholders orally and through actions and documents. Staff, students, and parents are able to articulate the vision or its implications and relationship to school goals.
Highly Effective
The vision is communicated systematically and clearly to and by nearly all stakeholders, both orally and through actions and documents. Staff, students, and parents articulate ownership of, and a belief in, the school's vision and related goals.

Component 2C: Creates or Maintains a Culture of Continuous Improvement

This component addresses how principals use data to drive school-wide priorities and goals, and how they create and implement plans to accomplish those goals. A culture of continuous improvement is not characterized by frantic activity — it is characterized by disciplined use of data, honest assessment of progress, and relentless follow-through. This component is directly connected to Region South's instructional leadership model and the data dashboard work that informs Region South's instructional leadership focus.

2C.1
Uses Data to Determine School-Wide Priorities and Goals
Classroom-level and school-wide data are used to assess the state of the school and determine goals aligned to the school's vision of high expectations; short- and long-term goals for grade levels, content areas, and target groups of students are based on multiple sources of data.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Classroom-level and school-wide data are used to assess the state of the school and determine goals aligned to the school's vision of high expectations.
Short- and long-term goals for grade levels, content areas, and target groups of students (English Learners, Students with Disabilities, Gifted Students) are based on multiple sources of data.
Learning expectations are consistent across groups of students.
At the Highly Effective level: Collaboratively and systematically seeks out and analyzes classroom-level and school-wide data and resource allocations to assess the state of the school; goals for each target group are based on disaggregated data; learning expectations are consistent across groups with differentiated expectations indicated as needed.
No evidence for Using Data to Determine School-Wide Priorities and Goals observed.
Region South connection: The Region South School Data Dashboard was built to support exactly this element — giving Directors and Administrators the school-level data needed to ask whether principals are setting goals grounded in iReady diagnostics, SBA results, attendance bands, and A-G completion rates. A principal who can speak to their specific iReady growth targets for English Learners by grade level is demonstrating Effective or Highly Effective practice on 2C.1.
Examples of Evidence
Analysis of state, district, and locally-designed assessment data (SBA, iReady, formative assessments)
Preparation of SPSA; SPSA Needs Assessment
Data-based rationale for school and sub-group goals
Use of additional data sources: surveys, needs assessments, interviews, focus groups
Schedule of data collection and plan review to determine meeting of benchmarks
CA School Dashboard, LCAP, TSP Plan; Data Walls
School Experience Survey including data disaggregated by subgroup
Special Education data reports
Ineffective
There is minimal to no evidence that classroom-level or school-wide data are used to assess the state of the school or establish goals. Goals for grade levels or content areas are absent or not based on data. Learning expectations are either not articulated or are lower for certain groups of students.
Developing
Limited or anecdotal classroom-level and school-wide data are used to assess the state of the school and determine goals aligned to the school's vision. Short- or long-term goals for grade levels or content areas are based on limited or aggregate data. Learning expectations are not consistent across groups of students.
Effective
Classroom-level and school-wide data are used to assess the state of the school and determine goals aligned to the school's vision of high expectations. Short- and long-term goals for grade levels, content areas, and target groups of students (e.g., English Learners, Students with Disabilities, Gifted Students) are based on multiple sources of data. Learning expectations are consistent across groups of students.
Highly Effective
Collaboratively and systematically seeks out and analyzes classroom-level and school-wide data and resource allocations to assess the state of the school and determine goals aligned to the school's vision of high expectations. Short- and long-term goals for grade levels, content areas, and each target group of students are based on disaggregated data. Learning expectations are consistent across groups of students, with differentiated expectations indicated as needed.
2C.2
Implements Plans to Accomplish School-Wide Goals
Collaboratively creates and implements plans for achieving school-wide goals that support the vision; uses the goals to guide decisions and monitor progress; resolves challenges to ensure that learning expectations and goals are met. Staff and students can articulate school-wide goals and progress made toward goal attainment.
Look-Fors — Evidence of Effective Practice
Collaboratively creates and implements plans for achieving school-wide goals that support the vision.
Uses the goals to guide decisions and monitor progress.
Resolves challenges to ensure that learning expectations and goals are met.
Staff and students can articulate school-wide goals and progress made toward goal attainment.
At the Highly Effective level: Collaboratively creates and implements systematic plans for achieving school-wide and disaggregated goals; anticipates and proactively addresses challenges; nearly all stakeholders can articulate school-wide goals and progress made toward goal attainment.
No evidence for Implementing Plans to Accomplish School-Wide Goals observed.
Examples of Evidence
Strategic, explicit plan for accomplishing goals with benchmarks, timelines, and action items
Observation of staff, students, and parents articulating their progress toward the school's goals
Records of faculty, teams, ILTs, PLCs analyzing student work and data to determine progress toward school-wide goals
Evidence of school-wide rubrics and exemplars where appropriate to assess individual and group attainment of school-wide goals
System of communication where all stakeholders hear about progress: online, data walls, verbal messages at school meetings
PDSA or PDR2; observation notes from an awards assembly
Ineffective
Does not create or implement plans for achieving school-wide goals, or plans are superficial; decisions are made without reference to goals; fails to monitor progress or resolve challenges to goal attainment. Few staff can articulate school-wide goals or progress made toward goal attainment.
Developing
Creates and implements plans for achieving school-wide goals that align with the vision; uses the goals to guide some decisions and monitor progress; attempts to resolve challenges to goal attainment, with uneven results. Some staff can articulate school-wide goals and progress made toward goal attainment.
Effective
Collaboratively creates and implements plans for achieving school-wide goals that support the vision; uses the goals to guide decisions and monitor progress; resolves challenges to ensure that learning expectations and goals are met. Staff and students can articulate school-wide goals and progress made toward goal attainment.
Highly Effective
Collaboratively creates and implements systematic plans for achieving school-wide and disaggregated goals that support the vision; uses the goals to guide decisions and monitor progress; anticipates and proactively addresses challenges to ensure that learning expectations and goals are met. Nearly all stakeholders can articulate school-wide goals and progress made toward goal attainment.
